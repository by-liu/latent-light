Full stack often means connecting the frontend and backend of a software
product. For AI device builders, I think the next full stack brings together
**AI + software + hardware**.

Imagine a small assistant that can hear a request, recognize an object and
respond. A model supplies the learned capability. Software moves data and
coordinates behavior. Hardware captures signals, runs computation and produces
the response within limits of memory, timing, energy and heat. Each layer
changes what the others can do.

That is the ambition behind this tutorial: **learn across the stack to turn an
AI idea into a useful device**. Hardware foundations give us a way to understand
those connections, ask better design questions and start building.

The scale matters. A **component** is a part used in a larger design. An
**integrated circuit**, or chip, implements circuits on semiconductor material;
its package provides physical connections. A **printed circuit board**, or PCB,
connects chips and other parts through copper traces, planes and connectors.
A **module** packages a reusable subsystem, often including a processor and
memory. A development board adds conveniences for experimentation.

A finished device adds an enclosure, an energy source, physical interaction
and a way to recover and update it. A connected system adds networks and
services. A capability demonstrated on a desk still needs those layers before
it becomes a dependable object people can use.

Keep this distinction when comparing names. Cortex-M33 is a CPU core design;
RP2350 is a microcontroller chip; Pico 2 is a board built around that chip.
The board includes flash and connections beyond the CPU itself.[^pico]

## Electricity and digital signals

### Voltage, current, resistance and power

**Voltage**, measured in volts (V), is an electrical potential difference between
two points. **Current**, measured in amperes (A), is the rate of charge flow.
A circuit needs a return path. A board's ground is its chosen reference and
return network; it is not necessarily a connection to the earth.

A resistor limits current according to its resistance, measured in ohms (Ω).
For an ideal resistor, Ohm's law relates the voltage across it to the current
through it. Electrical power is the rate of energy transfer, measured in
watts (W). For a steady DC load:[^electric-power]

<MathExpression display number="1" tex="V=IR, \qquad P=VI" />

Here *V* is voltage, *I* is current, *R* is resistance and *P* is power. If we
place **3.3 V across an ideal 10 kΩ resistor**, the current is
3.3 / 10,000 = **0.33 mA** and the resistor dissipates
3.3 × 0.00033 = **1.089 mW**. This is a calculation for a resistor;
a processor or radio changes its current as it works.

Watch the prefixes: **milli** is one thousandth, **micro** one millionth,
**kilo** one thousand and **mega** one million. Lowercase *b* means bits;
uppercase *B* means bytes. A byte contains eight bits.

A **power rail** is a supply distributed to a set of loads. A regulator converts
or controls the incoming supply to provide a required rail. A board can use
several rail voltages. The voltage printed beside a board's power connector
does not establish the allowed voltage on its signal pins.

A **capacitor** stores energy in an electric field and resists sudden changes
in its voltage. Local decoupling capacitors help supply changing current near
a chip. Their values, placement and connection paths matter; a long wire to
a distant supply does not replace the manufacturer's decoupling design.[^capacitors][^board]

### A bit is an agreement about a physical signal

Digital hardware interprets voltage ranges as logic 0 and logic 1. Between
the guaranteed low and high input ranges lies a region where neither value
is guaranteed. Output drive levels and input thresholds must leave a noise
margin. “Digital” simplifies interpretation; the wires still have resistance,
capacitance, noise and finite transition times.[^digital]

A **transistor** can control current and serve as a switch. Logic gates combine
such switches to perform Boolean operations. Storage elements retain bits;
a clock coordinates changes in many synchronous circuits. Clock frequency
counts cycles per second, not completed model operations. Work per cycle,
parallelism, memory stalls and the instruction sequence all affect the result.

A general purpose input/output pin, or **GPIO**, can observe a logic state or
drive a limited load. A motor, speaker or substantial light source usually
needs a suitable driver or amplifier. Read the pin's voltage, current and
operating-mode limits rather than treating it as a power supply.

The datasheet separates **recommended operating conditions** from **absolute
maximum ratings**. The latter are stress boundaries, not a promise of correct
operation. ST explicitly makes this distinction in its MCU datasheet.[^ratings]
When joining two boards, check signal thresholds, pin tolerance, return paths
and power states as well as connector fit.

## From physical signals to model inputs

A sensor converts a physical quantity into an electrical representation.
A microphone senses pressure changes; an image sensor converts light; an
inertial sensor measures acceleration or angular motion. A useful model needs
a representation that preserves the information relevant to its task.

An analog signal can vary continuously. An **analog-to-digital converter**
(ADC) samples it at selected times and quantizes its amplitude into discrete
codes. Amplification and filtering often precede conversion. A sensor with a
digital interface may contain those stages inside its package.[^sampling]

### Sampling rate and bit depth answer different questions

The sampling rate determines **when we observe** the signal. Bit depth determines
**how many amplitude codes we can represent**. For an ideal *n*-bit ADC spanning
a voltage range <MathExpression tex="V_{\mathrm{range}}" />, one nominal code step is:[^quantization]

<MathExpression display number="2" tex="q=\frac{V_{\mathrm{range}}}{2^n}" />

An ideal 12-bit ADC spanning 3.3 V has 4,096 codes and a nominal step of about
**0.806 mV**. This does not give the complete system that accuracy. Reference
error, noise, offset, gain, nonlinearity and temperature still matter.[^adc-errors]

For ordinary baseband sampling, the signal must be band limited below half
the sampling rate to permit unambiguous ideal reconstruction. A practical
anti-alias filter needs room to attenuate unwanted higher frequencies.
Sampling faster helps only when the rest of the signal path preserves what
we want.[^sampling] [Figure 4](#figure-4) shows why a model cannot recover
an ambiguity the acquisition process has already created.

### Preserve timing, units and the acquisition conditions

A stream is more than an array. Record the sample rate, physical units,
calibration, timestamp meaning and missing-data policy. For several sensors,
distinguish the time of exposure or sampling from the time software received
the buffer. Otherwise two individually valid streams can describe different
moments. These are proposed design habits, especially useful when debugging
multimodal systems.

Before choosing a model, estimate the raw traffic. For tightly packed,
uncompressed image payload:

<MathExpression display number="3" tex="R_{\mathrm{bytes/s}}=\frac{W H f c b}{8}" />

*W* and *H* are pixels per frame, *f* is frames/s, *c* is channels per pixel,
and *b* is bits per channel. **1280 × 720 × 30 × 3 × 8 / 8 = 82,944,000 bytes/s**,
or **82.944 MB/s**, for RGB at eight bits per channel. Transport overhead,
blanking and padding are excluded; a camera's packed RAW output can have a
different format. A double buffer alone holds two frames, here **5.5296 MB**.

For comparison, mono audio at **16,000 samples/s × 16 bits/sample / 8** carries
**32 kB/s** before framing. A small output classification can therefore depend
on a much larger continuous input. Deciding where to crop, filter, compress
or discard data is a hardware and software design decision.

## Understand the chip

### Separate execution engines from complete chips

A **CPU** executes a flexible instruction stream: arithmetic, branches, loads,
stores and control. A core is one execution engine within a processor design;
a multicore chip has several. The **instruction set architecture**, or ISA,
is the contract software targets, such as Arm or RISC-V. It does not by itself
specify a chip's clock speed, memory or peripherals.

A **microcontroller**, or MCU, integrates CPU cores with memory and peripherals
for controlling embedded work. A **system on a chip**, or SoC, combines several
functions on a chip. These terms overlap: an MCU is an integrated system, and
a larger application SoC may combine application CPUs, a control core,
accelerators, memory controllers and interfaces. NXP's i.MX 93 is a concrete
example of these roles coexisting.[^imx93]

[Figure 5](#figure-5) separates the board boundary from the chip boundary and
its execution engines. Use it to locate the part a specification describes.

**Figure 5.** Board, chip and execution engines are different boundaries. Original conceptual diagram; integration varies by platform.[^cuda-model][^imx93]
</HardwareFigure>

| Engine or block | What it contributes | What to ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CPU | Flexible control and general computation | Which cores, instructions, memory and operating environment? |
| GPU | Many parallel computations, including model kernels | Is the workload large and regular enough to use it well? |
| NPU | Specialized neural-network execution | Which operations, shapes and numeric formats does the toolchain support? |
| DSP / vector unit | Repeated signal and vector arithmetic | Which kernels use it, and where does their data live? |
| ISP / codec | Image processing or media encode/decode | Does it remove work or copies from the actual input path? |
| FPGA | Reconfigurable digital logic | Does custom timing or dataflow justify the design and toolchain effort? |

This table is a map of roles, not mutually exclusive product categories. A DSP
can be a separate processor or a capability within a CPU. A GPU can contain
special matrix units. An MCU can include an NPU: STM32N657 combines a Cortex-M55
CPU with ST's Neural-ART accelerator.[^stm-n6] CUDA likewise describes GPU
execution units and local storage within a larger heterogeneous system.[^cuda-model]

### Arithmetic has a representation and an execution cost

An AI layer repeatedly combines numbers, often through multiply-accumulate
operations: multiply inputs by weights and add the products. **INT8** represents
integers in eight bits. Floating point formats divide their bits among sign,
exponent and precision; their range and rounding behavior differ. Quantized
weights also need a mapping between stored codes and numerical values.

Storage precision, multiplication precision and accumulation precision can
differ. A four-bit checkpoint does not establish that every operation executes
as four-bit arithmetic. Read the actual backend contract; the
[quantization chapter](/edge-ai/quantization-compression/) develops this further.

**TOPS** means trillions of operations per second under the stated counting
rules. Before comparing it, ask which precision, whether sparsity is assumed,
how multiply-adds are counted and whether it describes one engine or the whole
chip. NVIDIA's Thor specifications, for example, explicitly label peak AI
figures as sparse FP4.[^jetson] Peak arithmetic says little about sensor
latency, unsupported operators, memory traffic or a hot enclosure by itself.

## Memory is a working material

A model needs storage for its file and memory for the work it performs.
ONNX Runtime's mobile guidance distinguishes fitting on disk from being
loadable into device memory.[^ort-mobile] Treat these as separate budgets.

[Figure 6](#figure-6) follows data from persistence to execution. The exact
hierarchy varies. A small MCU may primarily use on-chip SRAM and flash;
an application processor may add external DRAM and several cache levels.

<HardwareFigure id="figure-6" kind="memory">

**SRAM** is commonly used for on-chip working memory and caches. **DRAM**
provides denser working memory; **LPDDR** is a family of low-power DRAM
interfaces. **PSRAM**, or pseudo-static RAM, supplies external working memory
in embedded designs such as ESP32-S3 systems, with access restrictions that
depend on the platform.[^psram] **Flash** retains content without power. These
names describe different technologies and roles; “8 MB of flash” and “8 MB of
RAM” do not give the same working space.

### Weight payload is a starting point

For *N* parameters stored at one ideal packed bit width *b*:

<MathExpression display number="4" tex="M_{\mathrm{weights}}=\frac{Nb}{8}\;\mathrm{bytes}" />

**One billion parameters × four bits / eight = 500,000,000 bytes**,
or **0.5 GB ≈ 0.466 GiB**. Quantization metadata, padding and tensors at other
precisions increase the file or resident payload. Running the model also needs
activations, temporary workspace, input/output buffers, software allocations
and retained state. Autoregressive language models add a KV cache whose size
depends on architecture, context, batch and cache precision.

When assessing fit, identify each live allocation and its lifetime. Can the
runtime reuse a buffer after a layer finishes? Does the camera keep several
frames queued? Does a second model remain resident? Measure peak memory on
the intended path. The [Edge AI memory foundations](/edge-ai/foundations/)
provide a deeper accounting treatment.

### Capacity, bandwidth and latency are different constraints

Capacity asks **how much fits**. Bandwidth asks **how many bytes move per second**.
Latency asks **how long a particular access or completed operation takes**.
A large memory pool can still feed an engine too slowly.

The Roofline model relates arithmetic to traffic through a specified memory
level.[^roofline] An optimistic time bound for a kernel is:

<MathExpression display number="5" tex="t_{\mathrm{kernel}}\geq\max\!\left(\frac{F}{C},\frac{D}{B}\right)" />

*F* is the required operation count, *C* an applicable compute ceiling in
operations/s, *D* the bytes crossing the chosen memory boundary, and *B* its
bandwidth ceiling in bytes/s. This bound permits perfect overlap; it does
not predict actual time or include the whole application.

Suppose a hypothetical kernel needs **1 billion operations** and moves
**200 MB from DRAM**. At assumed ceilings of **1 TOPS** and **10 GB/s**, the
two terms are **1 ms** and **20 ms**. The optimistic bound is 20 ms.
More arithmetic capacity alone cannot remove that traffic limit. Reusing
values in local memory can reduce the bytes that cross the boundary.
These numbers are illustrative, not specifications or measured results.

Shared or **unified memory** can avoid some copies, but it does not remove
bandwidth contention or ordering requirements. MLX on Apple silicon lets CPU
and GPU use the same memory pool while its scheduler manages dependencies.
CUDA documents several different unified-memory behaviors, including cases
with migration. The phrase does not promise universal access to all installed
RAM by every engine.[^mlx][^cuda-memory]

## Connect the parts

An interface has several contracts: electrical levels, signaling and timing,
message format, software support and often a connector. Agreeing on one does
not establish the others. A physical connector can expose several possible
functions, and a pin may need configuration before it serves one of them.

| Connection | A useful first picture | Check before connecting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GPIO / PWM | Individual logic signals; pulse-width modulation controls pulse duty | Voltage, current, frequency and the required output driver |
| I²C | Shared clock and addressed data bus, often for sensor configuration | Addresses, pull-ups, bus capacitance, voltage and supported speed |
| SPI | Clocked transfers with chip selection, often for sensors, displays and flash | Clock polarity/phase, bit order, chip select and wiring limits |
| UART | Asynchronous serial data, often for logs or a peripheral | Baud, framing and electrical standard; UART pins are not automatically RS-232 |
| I²S / PDM | Common digital audio paths | Format, clocks, channel layout and microphone/codec support |
| MIPI CSI-2 / USB | Common camera transport paths | Sensor driver, pixel format, lanes or link speed, buffers and timing |
| PCIe / Ethernet / Wi-Fi / Bluetooth | Expansion or communication beyond a simple peripheral bus | Protocol roles, software, effective throughput, latency, power and recovery |

This is a functional guide, not a wiring specification. Standard/Fast-mode I²C uses
open-drain signaling and pull-ups; its electrical timing depends on the bus.
SPI uses a clock and device selection; UART configuration includes baud rate
and framing.[^i2c][^spi][^uart] NXP's i.MX 93 illustrates the separate camera,
audio, serial and network interfaces an application processor can expose.[^imx93]

**USB Type-C** names a connector system. The data protocol and USB Power
Delivery support are separate capabilities. A matching plug does not prove
a link's data rate or available power.[^usb] Check the board, cable, peripheral
and power source together.

For a first sensor connection, establish one modest transaction before adding
AI: read a documented identity or status register, interpret one sample with
known units, then sustain the target rate. A logic analyzer helps inspect
transactions; an oscilloscope reveals the underlying voltage and timing.
A packet decoder cannot tell you everything about a poor electrical waveform.

## Software owns time

### Firmware, drivers and operating systems

**Firmware** is the software that starts and operates the embedded system.
At boot, hardware and startup code establish memory, clocks and peripheral
state before the application can use them. A **driver** implements the contract
between software and a hardware block. A board support package, or BSP,
collects platform-specific support.

A **bare-metal** program can run a loop and interrupt handlers without a task
scheduler. An **RTOS** adds tasks, priorities and synchronization. A Linux system
adds a richer process, memory and driver environment. These are choices about
resources and software requirements; they do not map perfectly onto chip labels.
The i.MX 93, for example, lists both Linux and FreeRTOS support.[^imx93]

An **interrupt** asks the CPU to respond to an event. **Direct memory access**
(DMA) lets a hardware controller transfer data without the CPU copying every
item. Software still configures the operation, manages buffer ownership and
handles completion. On a cached platform, CPU and DMA views can require
explicit coherency handling; ST documents this issue with concrete examples.[^cache-dma]

For continuous audio, two buffers can alternate: acquisition fills one while
software processes the other. The producer must not overwrite a buffer still
in use. Decide what happens when processing falls behind: drop old data,
reduce work, flag a missed deadline or stop. An unbounded queue can turn a
fast individual inference into a stale response.

### A deadline is a system property

Throughput counts completed work per time. Latency measures how long one
item takes from a defined start to a defined end. Jitter describes variation
in timing. A real-time requirement means completing the required response
within its deadline, with the necessary confidence about worst cases.

A priority scheduler helps organize this work. FreeRTOS runs the highest
priority ready task in its documented single-core scheduling model.[^rtos]
It cannot make an overloaded design meet a deadline merely because the
software uses an RTOS. Interrupt execution, blocking, memory contention and
other tasks still enter the timing analysis.

As a hypothetical example, capture, preprocessing, inference and output taking
**10 + 3 + 12 + 2 ms** give **27 ms** on a serial path before additional waiting.
Pipelining separate resources may improve the completion rate while leaving
an item's latency at least as long as its own required path. Shared resources
and queues can make both worse. Timestamp the complete path and inspect the
slow cases, especially before using AI in a feedback loop.

### A model must become an executable device workload

Training produces parameters and a graph. Deployment must turn that graph
into supported operations, tensor layouts, memory allocations and kernels.
The runtime and compiler determine which engines execute which parts.

ONNX Runtime asks execution providers which subgraphs they can handle and
can fall back to a lower-priority provider for other nodes.[^ort] This makes
“the device has an NPU” an incomplete compatibility answer. Check operations,
shapes, numerical formats and the actual runtime version; inspect partitioning
and transfers, then measure the complete result. See
[runtimes, compilers and kernels](/edge-ai/runtimes-compilers/) for the deeper path.

## Power, heat and the physical device

### Energy determines endurance; peak power determines feasibility

Power in watts is a rate. Energy in watt-hours (Wh) is an amount:
**1 Wh = 3,600 joules**. Ampere-hours (Ah) describe charge; converting to
energy requires voltage. A nominal **2 Ah at 3.7 V** corresponds to about
**7.4 Wh**, before usable-capacity and conversion losses. Compare energy
on the same side of the power-conversion path.

A device moves among states: asleep, sensing, computing, transmitting and
interacting. If *dᵢ* is the fraction of time in each mutually exclusive state
and *Pᵢ* is that state's total power at the same measurement boundary:

<MathExpression display number="6" tex="P_{\mathrm{avg}}=\sum_i d_iP_i,\qquad\sum_i d_i=1" />

A hypothetical system at **1 W for 10% of the time** and **0.01 W for 90%**
averages **0.109 W**, excluding any transition costs not already included.
Concurrent radio, sensor and compute activity needs a combined state or a
separate accounting model; don't double count overlapping durations.

For nominal battery energy *E* in Wh, assumed conversion efficiency
<MathExpression tex="\eta" />, and average regulated-load power
<MathExpression tex="P_{\mathrm{load}}" /> in W:

<MathExpression display number="7" tex="t_{\mathrm{ideal}}\approx\frac{\eta E}{P_{\mathrm{load}}}\;\mathrm{hours}" />

At an assumed **85% efficiency**, **10 Wh** serving a **2 W load** gives
**4.25 hours** as an ideal energy budget. Actual usable energy and efficiency
change with the battery, load, temperature, aging and cutoff. TI's regulator
guidance treats input range, output voltage, maximum current and efficiency
as explicit design inputs.[^buck]

The average does not size the entire power path. A radio burst, motor start
or compute transient can exceed a rail's current capability and disturb its
voltage. Examine regulator response, decoupling and the supply path under
those events. A device with ample stored energy can still reset when a
peak load arrives.

### Heat changes what the device can sustain

Electrical energy consumed by computation largely becomes heat. Heat must
travel through the package, board, spreaders, enclosure and surrounding air.
An open development board with a fan does not establish performance inside
a small sealed product.

Junction temperature, board temperature, enclosure surface temperature and
ambient temperature are different quantities. TI explains why a datasheet's
junction-to-ambient thermal metric depends on the test board and environment;
it is not a universal package constant.[^thermal] NVIDIA documents thermal
sensing, fan control and clock throttling on Jetson Thor.[^jetson-power]
Measure the workload after the system reaches a representative sustained
condition, along with its power, temperature and quality.

Physical design also changes sensing and communication: microphone placement,
camera optics, mechanical vibration, antenna surroundings and connector strain
belong in the prototype. A smaller model cannot repair every failure caused
by a poor physical interface.

## Read the contract, then build

### A datasheet is more useful than a headline specification

Read the **block diagram** first to identify functions and paths. Then check
the **pinout** and package, memory map, electrical characteristics and timing.
A peripheral shown in the diagram may share pins with another function, or
require external memory, a clock, a PHY or a driver before it becomes usable.

| Document | What to extract for your prototype |
| --- | --- |
| Datasheet | Exact part/package, operating conditions, pins, electrical limits and tested conditions |
| Reference manual | Registers, peripheral behavior, clock tree, DMA and memory access paths |
| Errata | Known deviations and workarounds for the specific silicon revision |
| Hardware design guide | Power sequencing, decoupling, reset, clocks, layout and debug connections |
| Board schematic / manual | What this board actually connects, powers and exposes |
| SDK / runtime documentation | Supported board and model path, compiler/driver versions and examples |

Treat “typical” values as the stated characterization, not a guaranteed maximum.
Read the footnotes beside min/max values and measurement conditions. Check
whether a number belongs to the chip, module, development kit or total device.
ST's hardware integration guide is a useful concrete example: supply, reset,
boot and debug are separate design concerns around the MCU.[^board]

### Bring up one layer at a time

A useful first prototype answers a question you can measure. Choose a
supported development board or module, one sensor and an observable output.
You can apply this sequence to many kinds of device:

1. **Define the response.** State what the device observes, what it should do,
   the deadline and an acceptable failure behavior. Sketch information, power
   and control paths; estimate memory, traffic and energy.
2. **Establish the board.** Use the documented power connection, verify rails,
   boot, a console and a debug path. Confirm reset and recovery before adding
   a complex application.
3. **Establish the signal.** Read the sensor, verify units and timing against
   a known condition, then collect a sustained stream with a missing-data counter.
4. **Add a baseline, then AI.** Test a simple rule or known input/output first.
   Convert the model, compare its outputs with the reference implementation,
   and inspect which hardware executes it.
5. **Measure the complete device.** Include acquisition, preprocessing, queues,
   inference and output. Record power at a named boundary, peak memory and
   sustained temperature. Repeat under the physical conditions that matter.

Keep a small observation sheet: board and silicon revision, firmware and
runtime versions, model and numerical format, sensor settings, power mode,
ambient conditions, measurement method and workload. A multimeter checks
rails and steady quantities; a scope checks waveforms and transients; a
logic analyzer checks digital transactions; software tracing checks execution.
Choose the instrument for the question. These are proposed learning practices,
not a claim that measurements have been run for this article.

### Plan how the device will keep working

A custom PCB introduces layout, assembly, sourcing and test requirements.
Keep debug access, test points and recovery paths. Prove a questionable
interface or power assumption before committing it to a board layout.
A development kit can validate software while leaving those product questions open.

Updates also consume hardware resources. ESP-IDF's documented application OTA
path uses separate application slots and supports first-boot validation and
rollback. Other partitions have different interruption risks.[^ota] Budget
persistent storage for the recovery design you actually implement.

Secure boot verifies an authorized image; it does not establish that the
application behaves correctly. ESP32-S3's Secure Boot v2 documents signature
verification for the bootloader and application.[^secure-boot] Think about
identity, credentials, update authenticity and recovery while the memory and
boot design are still changeable. Add application checks for invalid inputs,
lost connectivity and failed sensing as well.

## A small platform map for 2026

The foundation applies across these roles. The examples below were checked
on **November 1, 2026**; some are established platforms, not 2026 releases.
They illustrate integration choices rather than purchase rankings or equivalent
performance. Choose with a supported workload and physical budget in hand.

The same broad constraints appear in larger accelerator systems, but building
an AI device starts with its sensing, response and physical operating budget.

## Check your understanding

Try answering these before looking at the suggested reasoning. They are
short design exercises, not measurements of particular products.

<details>
<summary>A camera board advertises an NPU and 8 MB of flash. Can our example RGB stream fit and run?</summary>

There is not enough information. Flash capacity does not establish working
RAM. One 1280 × 720 RGB frame needs **2.7648 MB** before padding; two need
**5.5296 MB**. Add the model, live buffers, activations, runtime and other
software. Then check transport rate, supported operations, timing and power.

</details>

<details>
<summary>A 10 Wh prototype averages 2 W but resets during radio transmission. Is a larger battery the first fix?</summary>

First observe the supply during the event. The ideal energy calculation says
nothing about transient current capability, voltage drop, regulator response
or wiring. Check the rail at the load and the documented power path. A larger
energy rating alone does not resolve the cause.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Inference takes 12 ms and frames arrive every 20 ms. Is the system real time?</summary>

The inference measurement covers only one stage. Include acquisition,
preprocessing, transfers, waiting and output; define the deadline and inspect
variation and worst cases under contention. A stable average arrival rate
does not establish that every response meets its deadline.

</details>
