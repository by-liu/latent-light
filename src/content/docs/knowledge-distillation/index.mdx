Knowledge distillation trains a **student model using supervision from a teacher**.
The teacher might provide an answer, probabilities over possible answers, or
internal representations. The transfer is learned through training, not simply
by shrinking the teacher's parameter file.[^hinton][^fitnets]

The useful question is broader than “How do we make a model smaller?” It is:
**Which capability should transfer, through what signal, and how will we know
the student learned it?** Students can even have the same architecture and size
as their teachers, as studied in Born Again Networks.[^born-again]

## The idea in one minute

A correct answer tells the student **what to predict**. A probability
distribution also exposes alternatives. An explanation can expose a route to
the answer. Teacher feedback on the student's own partial response can address
states that polished demonstrations never visit. These are different learning
signals, not interchangeable names for the same procedure.[^seqkd][^gkd][^step-by-step]

Three distinctions organize this collection:

- **Signal:** generated text, token probabilities, features or relationships.
- **Experience:** reference prefixes, teacher responses, current student
  responses, or replayed responses from an older student.
- **Objective:** the discrepancy or task criterion used to update the student.

We use this as an editorial map, not a universal taxonomy. Teacher access,
student architecture and available compute constrain which combinations are
practical. “On policy” describes whose behavior supplies the training
experience; it does not uniquely specify a loss.[^gkd]

## What this research asks beyond compression

**What actually transfers?** A student that sounds more helpful may not know
more. Gudibande et al.'s imitation experiments exposed this distinction in
their tested models; their conclusion is not a verdict on every later
distillation method.[^imitation]

**Which “best method” fits the setting?** We compare evidence under its teacher
access, student size, task and budget. A recent method beating its evaluated
baselines is not a universal state of the art.

**Does the complete system improve?** Better task accuracy can be offset by
longer responses, expensive teacher queries or a deployment backend that does
not exploit the smaller architecture. The [Edge AI collection](/edge-ai/)
examines the deployment side; this collection examines the transfer itself.
