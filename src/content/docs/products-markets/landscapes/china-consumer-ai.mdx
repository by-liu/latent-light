China’s consumer AI ecosystem is more varied than a list of chatbots. It includes
large-platform assistants, model-backed providers, creative tools, playable
content, personal knowledge products, social identities
and conversational hardware. Many sell globally; some also serve professionals
or enterprise customers.

This page brings together **26 company groups**, including one explicitly marked
availability watchlist. English and Chinese names make the directory useful for
both product discovery and entity checks. The regional lens describes documented
origin or operations, not a founder’s inferred ethnicity or audited customer mix.

**The useful contrast:** making something, representing a person and caring for a
physical companion create different reasons to return. They also create different
costs, safety questions and routes to market.

## Product patterns

[Figure 1](#figure-1) separates the experiences before comparing companies.
The evaluation questions are my synthesis, not measured advantages.

| Lens | What it does | A useful test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Moving images** | Create and direct video from prompts, images and references. | Can the creator control and finish the result? |
| **Editable 3D assets** | Turn a prompt or image into an asset for another workflow. | Is the asset useful after export, not only in the preview? |
| **Learning into making** | Bring material together, develop understanding and create personal work. | Does the workflow preserve evidence and the user's intent? |
| **Interactive worlds** | Create, play and remix experiences; the maker and player are different users. | Does making more content give people a reason to play? |
| **Personal tools and identity** | Coordinate workflows or represent a person using memory and context. | Who controls actions, disclosures and the representation? |
| **Conversational objects** | Put interaction into a familiar physical object. | Do controls, connectivity and support fit the household? |

Software creation and companion hardware have different users, distribution channels and operating costs.

### Large platforms: assistance becomes part of an existing ecosystem

ByteDance’s Doubao voice launch connects model work to a consumer conversation
experience. Tencent describes Yuanbao’s artifact-generating Agent function.
Alibaba’s Qwen App announcement connects requests to commerce, travel and payments,
with explicit confirmation for Alipay transactions.[^broad-doubao][^broad-yuanbao][^broad-qwen]

DeepSeek and Kimi are model-backed application providers rather than small vertical
tools. Baidu is included as an ecosystem comparator, but its dynamic landing page
did not expose enough detail for a workflow or permission comparison. MiniMax
spans creation, conversation and developer services; a model name, an app and the
company are three different units.[^broad-deepseek][^broad-kimi][^broad-baidu][^broad-minimax]

**My interpretation:** domestic service integration and global distribution are
different strategic questions. Study where a product can actually complete work,
which account and payment rails it uses, and what the person must still approve.
Do not compare a company-wide user disclosure with a specialist app’s paying cohort.

### Creation: from generating a clip to finishing a project

PixVerse and Vidu combine self-service creation with professional and API
channels. Evoken groups LiblibAI, Xingliu and LibTV under one parent. VAST’s
Tripo adds 3D creation, while SeaArt combines creative tools with community
experiences.[^dir-pixverse][^dir-vidu][^dir-evoken][^dir-tripo-product][^dir-seaart-filing]

The interesting product question is not simply which generator looks best in a
demo. It is how people control characters, preserve an intended style, revise
the work and use the result. Hobby expression, social sharing, advertising and
production assets have different quality and rights requirements.

**What to study:** editing friction, consistency across a project, successful
exports and usage rights. Treat company-reported output or user counts as
dated disclosures, not comparable active-user or revenue measures.

### Interactive content: the maker and the player are different users

SEELE combines game creation with a workspace and community. Loopit describes
prompt-created interactive content. PixVerse’s July 2026 announcement also
outlines a move toward interactive entertainment.[^dir-seele][^dir-loopit][^dir-pixverse]

Generating more content can help creators without creating a reason for players
to return. That distinction changes product priorities: discovery, remixing,
shared experiences, stable rules and moderation may matter as much as generation.

**What to study:** repeat play by people who did not create the experience,
creator-to-player conversion and how often an experience remains useful after
the first try. SEELE’s stated future play/social monetization should not be
presented as an already operating revenue channel.[^dir-seele]

### Learning and personal research: useful structure around your material

OralCraft focuses on English speaking practice. MetaSo connects search,
learning and files; ListenHub turns material into narrated formats. YouMind
links source collection, exploration and editable outputs.[^dir-oral-product][^dir-metaso][^dir-listenhub][^youmind]

These products promise different forms of progress: speaking more confidently,
finding grounded information, understanding a source or making an artifact.
A polished summary does not establish all four.

**What to study:** whether results remain faithful to their sources, whether
people can correct and trace them, and whether the workflow improves ability
or understanding outside the app. Material saved is not the same as material
understood.

### Personal AI: represent me, help me, or keep me company?

Second Me emphasizes personal identity and social representation. Macaron builds
memory-informed personal tools with connected actions. EVE describes a 3D
companion but remains an availability watchlist in this study. Flowith’s
multi-step creative work is another distinct use of the agent label.[^dir-secondme][^dir-macaron][^dir-macaron-policy][^dir-eve][^dir-flowith]

**What to study:** the responsibility being delegated. Remembering preferences
is different from representing someone in a conversation, sharing information
with another service or providing a relationship. Each needs a different
permission, correction and departure experience.

## Conversational hardware has a different operating model

Haivivi’s toys, FoloToy’s configurable devices, Ropet’s care-and-play experience
and Robopoet’s Fuzozo extend interaction into a physical object. Hardware can
change where and how a conversation starts, but it also introduces delivery,
returns, connectivity and long-term service obligations.[^dir-haivivi][^dir-folotoy][^dir-ropet][^dir-robopoet-investor]

| Layer | What needs investigation |
| --- | --- |
| Device | Durability, charging, connectivity, returns and replacement |
| Ongoing service | Voice/model costs, subscriptions, support and service lifetime |
| Interaction | Interruptions, environment noise, latency and age-appropriate behavior |
| Trust | Recording controls, parent controls, deletion and changing model behavior |

This is a proposed product checklist, not a measured assessment of the listed
devices. A sale is not proof of recurring software revenue, and a reassuring
character design is not proof of safe behavior.

The US PIRG December 2025 report illustrates why safety evidence needs a version
and a date: it identified serious issues with FoloToy’s Kumma, then reported
that a retest no longer produced several of the originally observed problems.
Neither finding establishes that every current configuration is safe or that
the earlier risks did not occur.[^dir-toy-safety]
Ropet’s published policy also restricts certain AI features to users aged 16+;
“AI toy” should not automatically be read as appropriate for every child.[^dir-ropet-privacy]

## Identity and status

Two checks prevent misleading comparisons.

**Match the legal entity before attaching financing.** Second Me belongs to
Mindverse AI / 心识宇宙. Macaron is associated with 心洲科技 and uses a similar
English name in reporting. Their product and policy records identify different
operators. The nearly USD50M A round reported in June 2026 belongs to Macaron’s
parent, not Second Me.[^dir-mindverse-identity][^dir-macaron-policy][^dir-macaron-disclosure]

**Do not count a parent and each product as separately financed startups.**
Evoken’s three products share a company row. MiniMax’s Hailuo and Talkie also
share one provider row. Its January 2026 Hong Kong listing is why it is a
listed-company comparator, not another private-startup funding record. The broader
landscape intentionally includes both kinds of provider.[^dir-evoken][^dir-minimax][^broad-minimax]

The financing column preserves other important distinctions: PixVerse’s
USD439M refers to its whole Series C including an extension; Tripo’s RMB3B
combines B and B+; Haivivi’s A+ amount is undisclosed even though the announcement
mentions the earlier A round. Flowith’s reported total differs between press
and investor sources, so the amount remains unresolved.[^dir-pixverse][^dir-tripo-round][^dir-haivivi][^dir-flowith-funding][^dir-flowith-investor]

## Questions for the next research pass

- Which offers retain personal users beyond an initial creation or chat session?
- How much of each mixed company’s business actually comes from consumers?
- Which global products change pricing, moderation or data practices by market?
- Can users carry their creations and history elsewhere?
- What happens to a connected device if the service changes or disappears?
