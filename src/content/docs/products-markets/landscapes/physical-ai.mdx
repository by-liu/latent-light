A system that generates a convincing world is not necessarily a system that can
act in it. Physical AI is easier to understand when we separate representing an
environment, choosing actions, collecting experience, testing behavior and
operating the resulting machine.

This landscape connects global platforms with **China’s embodied AI ecosystem**.
It includes model developers, robot makers, warehouse and service automation,
and the tools that turn experiments into operating systems. Evidence was
inspected **October 10–11, 2026**. Research releases, product demonstrations,
commercial deployments and financing announcements remain distinct.

## What China adds to the landscape

China is not one humanoid category. The useful picture has **three overlapping
layers**: learning systems, robot bodies and commercial workflows. That grouping
is my interpretation of the inspected products, not a market-share estimate.

| Layer | Selected examples | What to compare |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Models, data and developer stacks** | X Square, Spirit AI, Galbot, Galaxea, Robbyant, Alibaba DAMO, ByteDance Seed, AGIBOT World, D-Robotics.[^xsquare][^spirit][^galbot-product][^galaxea][^robbyant-owner][^rynn][^seed][^agibot-world][^drobotics] | Action interface, data coverage, adaptation, license and supported hardware. |
| **Bodies and integrated systems** | Unitree, AGIBOT, UBTECH, LimX, EngineAI, Robotera, Fourier, Astribot, XPENG, DEEP Robotics and Leju.[^unitree-product][^agibot-product][^ubtech][^limx][^engine][^robotera][^fourier][^astribot][^xpeng-product][^deep-robotics][^leju] | Task success, supervision, recovery, maintenance and integration cost. |
| **Commercial workflow comparators** | Geek+, Hai Robotics, Pudu, KEENON, Flexiv and Dobot.[^geek-product][^hai][^pudu][^keenon][^flexiv][^dobot] | Completed work per shift, interventions, installed workflow and service support. |

### Models and bodies are becoming more closely connected

AGIBOT publishes model and data resources alongside its robot portfolio.
Galaxea exposes VLA deployment entrypoints for its R1 hardware. Leju’s LeTools
connects collection, training and deployment on Kuavo. These are concrete examples
of a hardware and learning loop, not proof that vertical integration is always
the better business model.[^agibot-product][^agibot-world][^galaxea][^leju]

**My interpretation:** integration can make data collection and testing easier to
coordinate, but it can also bind a model to a particular sensor setup, action
interface or robot body. Ask what transfers without new demonstrations, what
requires adaptation and who owns the failure when components are combined.

### The established businesses matter as much as the humanoid demos

Warehouse storage, cleaning, delivery and adaptive industrial arms are different
problems from a general-purpose household robot. Geek+, Hai Robotics, Pudu,
KEENON, Flexiv and Dobot provide useful comparisons because their products are
organized around identifiable workflows.[^geek-product][^hai][^pudu][^keenon][^flexiv][^dobot]

That does not independently prove their economics. It does give a more useful
question than “Which robot looks most capable?”: **What work is completed,
under what operating constraints, and with how much human help?**

### Open resources do not mean unrestricted deployment

AGIBOT World, GalaxeaVLA, LingBot and LeRobot expose different combinations of
data, models, code and tools.[^agibot-world][^galaxea][^lingbot][^lerobot]
Galaxea’s current repository explicitly distinguishes historical Apache-licensed
materials from newer community-licensed materials requiring separate permission
for commercial use.[^galaxea] Inspect the actual artifact and intended use,
not only an “open” label. Availability is also not evidence of safe robot control.

The earlier map underrepresented **robot makers and bounded industrial products**.
Figure, 1X, Apptronik and Agility add humanoid operating models. Sanctuary,
Dexterity and ANYbotics add dexterity, logistics and inspection. FieldAI,

## How to read financing without reading too much into it

The distinctions below change the meaning of a headline. They are not cosmetic.

| Disclosure | Example | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Named round** | LimX: Series B; D-Robotics: Series C.[^limx][^drobotics] | No later round, safe autonomy or positive unit economics. |
| **Committed capital** | Figure’s Series C announcement says committed capital.[^figure-funding] | That every dollar has already been received. |
| **Agreements signed** | XPENG robotics financing announcement describes share purchase agreements.[^xpeng-funding] | Independently confirmed closing or customer delivery. |
| **Securities sold** | Generalist’s Form D separates about \$198.2M sold from \$208.2M offered.[^generalist-sec] | A named Series stage or permission to add the offering target to proceeds. |
| **Public status** | Unitree, UBTECH, Geek+ and Dobot have listing evidence.[^unitree-listed][^ubtech][^geek-listed][^dobot] | General technical leadership or the same maturity across all product lines. |
| **Proposed transaction** | Agility’s public combination; World Labs’ AMD agreement.[^agility-status][^amd] | A completed listing or acquisition. |
| **Undisclosed / unverified** | Several private companies do not have a verified dated round in the inspected sources. | Zero funding, poor quality or proof that no round occurred. |

The funding column helps explain ownership and resources. The deployment column
helps explain what has actually been offered, released or reported. **Neither is
a substitute for the other.**

## A value chain with a feedback loop

[Figure 1](#figure-1) is a functional map, not a set of isolated markets. A provider
may span several roles. Deployment produces observations and failures that can
inform data selection, training and further tests; that feedback is my synthesis
of the inspected model and data-platform accounts.[^skild][^foxglove][^roboto]

| Lens | What it does | A useful test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Represent the world** | Construct or predict environments for creation, training and tests. | What geometry, physics and action responses are actually validated? |
| **Choose actions** | Map observations and goals to robot behavior. | Which robots, tasks and environments does the policy work on? |
| **Collect experience** | Build and annotate the demonstrations and interactions needed for learning. | Does more data add useful coverage or repeat familiar situations? |
| **Organize and diagnose** | Find events, investigate behavior and curate datasets across recordings. | Can an observed failure become a reproducible test and useful data? |
| **Validate and operate** | Combine policy tests, controlled hardware trials and monitored deployment. | What evidence justifies release, and what stops an unsafe run? |

Feedback: operation → recorded behavior → investigation and data selection → updated policy → fresh evaluation. The loop is not a guarantee of improvement.

## Four boundaries worth keeping clear

### A platform, a robot and an operator are different businesses

Google DeepMind’s VLA and embodied-reasoning models supply distinct capabilities.
NVIDIA supplies simulation infrastructure, robot policies and world models as
distinct layers.[^groot][^cosmos] Boston Dynamics markets Atlas for
industrial workflows; Tesla’s page describes the Optimus development goal.
Amazon’s June 2025 announcement reports its million-robot milestone and a
fleet-coordination model.[^broad-robotics][^isaac][^broad-atlas][^broad-tesla][^broad-amazon-robotics]

**My interpretation:** these are complementary roles, not interchangeable “robotics
companies.” Compare integration and model access for a platform, task performance
and serviceability for hardware, and throughput, intervention and cost for an
operator. A development goal, an early-adopter invitation and a deployed-fleet
disclosure are different evidence states.

### A world model and a policy have different jobs

A world model represents or predicts an environment. A policy chooses what the
robot should do. In Runway’s evaluation experiment, each policy chooses actions
in response to observations generated by the model. openpi exposes robot
observations and action outputs rather than only generating a visual scene.[^runway][^openpi]

“World model” therefore does not identify a complete product category. A
navigable creative scene, a physically based simulator and an action-conditioned
prediction used in a control loop have different jobs. World Labs’ Marble is
a spatial creation product; that alone does not establish validated contact
physics or robot-control performance.[^worldlabs]

The company also announced a definitive agreement to join AMD on September 28,
2026. The announcement expects closing by the end of 2026, subject to conditions.
An announced agreement is not a completed transaction.[^amd]

### Generality is conditional

Skild describes learning across robot bodies. Physical Intelligence releases
models that practitioners can adapt. Compare the supported body, observations,
action interface, task distribution and adaptation procedure, not only the
word “generalist.” The openpi warning that adaptation to a new robot may fail
is useful counterevidence to the assumption that a checkpoint works everywhere.[^skild][^openpi]

For an operator, the practical question is narrower: what does this system do
reliably on my hardware, in my environment, with my acceptable level of
supervision? A demonstration on another body is a lead, not the acceptance test.

### A good ranking is not a safety certificate

Runway’s February 2026 study evaluated **eight policies**, using **1,450 simulated
rollouts**, on RoboArena manipulation tasks with a **Franka Panda arm**. It
reports **0.95 Pearson correlation** between simulated and real policy scores.
The authors distinguish relative ranking from predicting absolute success rates.[^runway]

| What the reported result supports | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- |
| Comparing candidates within the studied setup | General transfer to every robot or task |
| A promising way to reduce some hardware evaluation work | Calibrated probabilities of failure |
| Agreement between simulated and real policy scores | Safe unattended deployment |

This interpretation follows the study’s stated scope and limitations, not a
claim of independent reproduction.[^runway] Correlation alone also cannot tell
an operator which rare failure modes were missed.

## Follow one failure through the value chain

Consider a robot that occasionally drops an object. The example below is an
illustrative workflow, not a reported incident or a guarantee that a provider
solves it.

| Step | Evidence needed | Role in the landscape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observe | Synchronized video, state, actions and deployment versions | Recording and data operations |
| Diagnose | Repeatable conditions and competing explanations | Search, visualization and analytics |
| Select data | Cases that add coverage, with evaluation held separate | Collection, annotation and curation |
| Compare policies | Reproducible tasks, interventions and failure severity | Simulation and policy evaluation |
| Release | Hardware evidence, operating limits and a recovery procedure | Integration and operation |

The product opportunity is to connect these steps. Surfacing an anomaly is
useful, but the operator still needs a reproducible case, a justified change
and evidence that it helped without introducing a regression. That connection
is my synthesis of the data, policy and evaluation roles, not a comparative
measurement of the listed companies.[^foxglove][^roboto][^openpi][^runway]

More data is not automatically the right next step. The failure could involve
perception, timing, calibration, grasp selection or changing objects. A useful
workflow helps choose between explanations before collecting a larger dataset
or training another model. Those are diagnostic questions, not asserted causes
of an actual failure.

## Evaluation is becoming a product category

SignalFlag orchestrates tests across existing simulation, replay and hardware
evidence; it does not create its own simulator.[^signalflag] Runway and One Robot
approach evaluation through learned environments. One
Robot describes task-specific simulations for hard manipulation such as
textiles and box folding. The retained Instance launch starts with detecting
whether a real rollout succeeded, with a broader automated judgment and reset
rig as its ambition. These address distinct bottlenecks: candidate comparison,
task fidelity, human judgment and hardware availability.[^runway][^onerobot][^instance]

| Evaluation product | What a buyer should ask |
| --- | --- |
| Learned simulation | Does it preserve the decisions that matter on real hardware? |
| Success detector | What are false-success rates, uncertain cases and transfer limits? |
| Test orchestration | Can a failure be reproduced across simulator, logs, software and hardware versions? |
| Automated test rig | Are resets correct, trials reproducible and failures safely handled? |
| Release evidence | Which conditions were tested, which remain excluded and who approves operation? |

These are proposed acceptance criteria. A launch description does not establish
independent performance. Most Robotic’s current YC profile also differs from
the retained Instance launch, so the table does not assume that the original
product or full rig is currently generally available.[^instance]

## Compare economics at the operator’s boundary

An API price or training benchmark captures only part of the cost. For a robot
operator, compare completed work under a stated task distribution, including
integration, downtime, interventions, resets, recovery and maintenance. A
successful trial with a nearby researcher is not the same operating model as
reliable work across a fleet.

For a data platform, measure the time from a recorded failure to a useful
diagnosis or dataset. For an evaluation tool, measure whether it selects better
candidates and avoids expensive wrong decisions, not just how many cheap
simulations it runs. For a collection service, measure useful coverage and
downstream gains rather than hours of data alone.

The key distinction is **better research throughput versus better operating
performance**. Faster experiments can be valuable without proving that a robot
is ready for unattended use. Both outcomes need evidence, but not the same
evidence. These are proposed product measures, not ROI results collected here.

## Open questions

- How much useful coverage comes from each additional demonstration or deployment?
- When does simulated evidence change a hardware testing decision correctly?
- Can a team reproduce a failure across data, policy, hardware and environment versions?
- Who owns integration, supervision, recovery and the final release decision?
- Which part of the improvement loop is expensive enough for a customer to pay to shorten it?
