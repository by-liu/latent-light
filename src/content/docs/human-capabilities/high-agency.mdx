Imagine wanting to organize a small community workshop. You could spend weeks
reading about events, waiting for a venue, or asking AI to produce increasingly
elaborate plans. You could also talk to three potential participants, find out
what they need, and propose a modest first session. The second approach creates
information that planning alone cannot supply.

That is the useful idea behind **high agency**: choosing a worthwhile direction,
looking for ways to influence what happens, taking an appropriate next step,
and learning from the result. This is a working definition for this guide,
combining the popular idea of resourcefulness with psychological accounts of
intentional action and self regulation.[^weinstein][^bandura]

**AI can expand your reach. Agency helps you turn that reach into useful action.**
That is the sense in which high agency can be a superpower in this era: you can
attempt more, work across a wider range of skills, and learn from real attempts.
The advantage depends on choosing a worthwhile goal and checking what happens.
This is the guide's interpretation of the evidence, not a demonstrated effect
of a single personality trait.[^aiwork][^frontier]

## What the term means

### A popular label with older foundations

One influential modern formulation appeared in Eric Weinstein's January 2016
conversation with Tim Ferriss. He described treating an apparent impossibility
as a prompt to look for another route, rather than automatically accepting the
first explanation of why something cannot be done. This establishes a familiar
usage of the term, not its ultimate origin or a scientific definition.[^weinstein]

The language is visible in contemporary technology culture. In February 2026,
WIRED reported that technology leaders were discussing human agency as a hiring
priority alongside AI adoption. That is evidence of attention in that community;
it does not measure public awareness, search growth, or a predictor of career
success.[^wired]

Psychology offers more precise concepts. Albert Bandura's account of human
agency includes forming intentions, anticipating consequences, regulating
action, and reflecting on one's capabilities and choices. His theory also
places people within social systems that they both influence and are influenced
by.[^bandura]

The popular phrase combines several ideas that research usually studies
separately. The comparison below is a synthesis, rather than a claim that these
constructs form a single validated high agency score.

| Related concept | Meaning and example |
| --- | --- |
| Self efficacy | Belief that you can perform the actions needed for a particular outcome: “I can learn to give this presentation with practice.”[^efficacy] |
| Personal initiative | Starting action, looking ahead, and persisting through obstacles: contacting the organizer and proposing a trial session.[^initiative] |
| Self regulation | Guiding action and reviewing it against your intentions: checking whether the session met participants' needs.[^bandura] |

Someone can feel capable yet never start. Someone else can begin while uncertain
and build confidence through experience. It is more useful to ask which part of
the process needs attention than to declare yourself a high or low agency person.

### What to look for in practice

For this guide, the useful signs are observable: a person clarifies the outcome,
finds a feasible next action, asks for help when appropriate, follows through,
and updates their approach when evidence changes. These are proposed ways to
notice the behaviour, not a diagnostic test.

Agency also involves deciding when to wait, obtain permission, change the goal,
or stop. A considered decision to protect health, respect another person's
boundaries, or abandon a poor project can be an exercise of agency. Neither
constant activity nor unlimited persistence is a sensible standard.

## Why it matters beyond AI

Initiative can turn an unclear problem into something you can learn about.
A conversation tests whether the workshop is useful. A first session tests
whether the format works. Feedback can improve the next decision. This is the
guide's central interpretation: purposeful action gives you a way to participate
in shaping events, while reflection helps you avoid repeating ineffective action.
It draws on agency theory and experimental evidence about monitoring goals.[^bandura][^monitoring]

There is evidence that some relevant behaviours can be developed. A randomized
study assigned 1,500 small business owners in Togo to personal initiative
training, traditional business training, or a control group. Over two years,
the initiative training increased profits by about 30% relative to control;
the traditional training estimate was 11% and was not statistically significant.
The intervention included 36 hours of training and subsequent individual visits.
The results concern a substantial programme for selected entrepreneurs.[^initiative]

That finding supports the value of structured initiative training in this
setting. It does not imply that adopting the label will improve anybody's income
by 30%. A follow up after roughly seven years found larger profit gains for men
than women. The investigators could not establish a single explanation for the
divergence. The same training can have different returns as opportunities and
constraints unfold.[^followup]

The broader case for agency is therefore practical: it gives you behaviours to
work on. It should not become a promise that effort determines every outcome.

## Why it can be a superpower in the AI era

The useful meaning of “superpower” here is **greater capacity to make things
happen**. A person who notices a problem, finds help, and follows through can
use AI to bridge some of the gaps between an intention and a result. The
combination matters because additional capability becomes valuable when you
put it to work toward something worthwhile.

[Figure 2](#figure-2) shows that relationship. The evidence supports particular
benefits of AI assistance; the connection to high agency is the synthesis
developed here. The studies below did not test whether people classified as
high agency receive larger benefits than others.

### Start with less friction

AI can help with drafting, explanation, analysis, and exploring alternatives.
In some settings, that assistance produces measurable gains. A study following
a staggered rollout of an AI assistant to 5,172 customer support agents found
about 15% more issues resolved per hour on average, with larger benefits for
less experienced workers. This was a workplace adoption study, not a randomized
test of every kind of work.[^aiwork]

For someone practising agency, the opportunity is to use assistance to get
past a specific obstacle. If you are stuck on an unfamiliar format, a first
draft or explanation can give you something to work with. This is a practical
inference from the assistance evidence: a lower cost of preparation can make
a first attempt more feasible.[^aiwork][^frontier]

For the workshop, AI might help you compare formats or draft an invitation.
Your next action becomes simpler: review the invitation and ask three people
whether the session would help them. Initiative turns the available assistance
into a concrete attempt.

### Work across more skills

Many useful projects involve several kinds of work: understanding a need,
explaining an idea, planning delivery, and evaluating results. AI assistance
can sometimes help people contribute beyond their usual specialism.

In a preregistered experiment at Procter & Gamble, the published analysis
covered 791 professionals working on product innovation. Individuals with AI
produced solutions of comparable quality to two person teams without AI.
AI users also proposed solutions that better balanced commercial and technical
perspectives across professional backgrounds. The experiment used a GPT‑4
tool with training in a one day workshop. It did not establish that one person
can replace a standing team.[^teammate]

One practical implication is to begin unfamiliar work with help: ask for an
explanation, prepare a draft, and bring specific questions to a knowledgeable
person. Agency includes finding support that lets you contribute.[^teammate]

### Learn from faster attempts

When assistance reduces preparation time, you may be able to test an idea
sooner. The important step is to connect the draft to a question you can answer
through action. For the workshop: would people attend this format, can they
use the explanation, and what would make the session more useful?

You might ask AI for two versions of an invitation, show them to potential
participants, and revise the clearer one. Their responses teach you something
about the audience that another round of generated suggestions cannot establish
on its own. A smaller first session can then inform the next one.

This is a proposed way to combine AI assistance with progress monitoring,
whose benefits have been studied experimentally. The complete AI assisted
loop shown here has not been validated as a training programme. Faster
preparation creates an opportunity for learning when you also seek and use
feedback.[^frontier][^monitoring]

### Put judgment where it matters

More ideas and more polished drafts give you more material to choose from.
Agency includes deciding which work deserves action, what to verify, and
when to change course. In the Procter & Gamble experiment, AI improved the
quality of generated ideas, but did not improve participants' ability to
select their best idea. Better material and better selection are separate
questions.[^teammate]

For the workshop, you still need to discover whether people want the session,
decide what you can responsibly offer, and see what they learn. A useful
exercise of agency is to define success before asking AI to accelerate the
work: “Participants can do this task themselves afterward.” That gives you
a reason to reject a polished explanation that fails to teach.

### Performance, understanding, and confidence can diverge

The strongest reason to pair initiative with judgment is that AI's effects vary.
These studies answer different questions; their numbers should not be combined
into one estimate of AI productivity.

**Professional work.** In a preregistered experiment with 758 consultants using
GPT‑4, assistance helped on tasks within the model's capabilities: participants
completed 12.2% more tasks and worked 25.1% faster. On one task beyond that
boundary, AI users were about 19 percentage points less likely to reach the
correct answer. Test the actual task and inspect substantive correctness,
even when the output looks professional.[^frontier]

**Learning.** In a randomized high school mathematics study in Turkey, students
practised with ordinary course resources, a general GPT‑4 chat interface, or
a customized GPT‑4 tutor. The tutor received teacher prepared solutions,
common mistakes, and hints, with instructions to help without giving the
complete answer. These were its tutoring safeguards.[^learning]

The AI groups did better during assisted practice. On subsequent exams without
assistance, the general interface group's grades were 17% lower relative to
control. The guarded tutor avoided that harm, but did not produce a demonstrated
exam gain. This measures performance after practice, not learning retained over
months or years. If the goal is learning, check what you can do without the
tool; completing assisted work does not establish the same result.[^learning]

**Confidence.** A survey of 319 workers found that higher confidence in AI was
associated with less reported critical thinking. This was self reported and
correlational, not proof that AI caused a lasting loss of ability. Treat
confidence in an answer as a reason to check your evidence, rather than as
evidence itself.[^critical]

The consulting and classroom results concern specific GPT‑4 systems and tasks.
They establish possibilities and failure modes; they do not locate the exact
limits of every current model. In February 2026, METR also explained why selection
bias and changing work patterns made its newer developer productivity estimates
hard to interpret. As tools change, old results and new anecdotes both need
careful handling.[^metr]

Human agency and an **AI agent** also describe different things. A software
system can carry out delegated steps. The person using it still makes choices
about purposes, acceptable tradeoffs, authorization, and how to judge the outcome.
Delegating more steps is not, by itself, a measure of better human judgment.

The evidence does not establish that being high agency makes a person immune
to job displacement. A more defensible goal is to become better at directing,
testing, and learning from the tools available to you.

## How to develop it

action, planning around obstacles, and checking progress. The loop is this guide's

### 1. Choose an outcome you actually value

Start with a concrete situation. “Become more agentic” does not tell you what
to do this afternoon. “Find out whether five neighbours want a beginner workshop”
does. Define a result you can recognize and explain why it matters to you or the
people involved.

A useful prompt is: **What would be different if this effort worked?**
If you cannot answer, spend a little time clarifying the goal before accelerating
the work. You can also discover that a goal borrowed from someone else is not
worth pursuing.

Ryan and Deci's self-determination theory distinguishes **autonomous motivation**,
acting willingly for reasons you endorse, from **controlled motivation**, acting
under pressure such as fear of disapproval. Autonomy concerns your endorsement
of an action; it does not require independence from other people. You can value
an outcome even when a particular task is tedious.[^motivation]

For the workshop, filling in a venue form may be uninteresting but worthwhile
because accessible learning matters to you. This original example illustrates
the distinction: ask whether the effort serves a value you endorse, while
recognizing that meaningful duties can involve routine work and cooperation.

### 2. Separate action from influence and constraint

Make a short, practical map:

- **Actions available now:** prepare questions, contact a willing participant,
  or learn a missing skill.
- **Outcomes you can influence:** whether someone attends, how a proposal is
  received, or whether a partner helps.
- **Constraints requiring support or a different approach:** budget, access,
  caring responsibilities, health, authority, or institutional rules.

This map is a planning aid proposed here. Its purpose is to find a legitimate
next move while recognizing what you cannot unilaterally determine. A constraint
may call for resources, collective action, a smaller project, or a pause.

### 3. Build capability through manageable attempts

Choose a step small enough to attempt but substantial enough to teach you
something. For a presentation, explain the idea to one person and ask what they
understood. For the workshop, run a short trial before committing to a full series.

Self efficacy theory identifies successful experience and observing others as
sources of capability beliefs. That supports a practical emphasis on building
experience and learning from examples. Confidence also needs calibration: some
self efficacy measures may mix perceived ability with motivation, a live debate
reviewed by the American Psychological Association.[^efficacy]

Keep the question specific: **What can I now do more reliably?** A record of
actual attempts is more informative than a global declaration of confidence.

### 4. Plan for the obstacle you expect

One studied method combines **mental contrasting** with **implementation
intentions**. You imagine a desired future, identify an obstacle in present
reality, and form an “if this happens, then I will do that” plan. A meta analysis
of 21 studies found a small to moderate average improvement in goal attainment.
Effects varied, and the authors noted that publication bias could inflate the
estimate.[^planning]

An example proposed here is: “If I start rewriting the invitation again, I will
send the current version to one trusted person for feedback.” The response
addresses a specific obstacle. Pick a feasible goal and a response you can
actually carry out; a plan cannot supply missing money, authority, or time.

### 5. Ask for help and obtain real feedback

You do not need to be self sufficient to exercise agency. Bandura distinguished
personal action, relying on others to act on your behalf, and collective action.
Working with other people is part of the picture.[^bandura]

Ask a clear question that another person can answer: “Could you tell me which
part of this explanation is confusing?” or “Who can approve use of this room?”
Make requests respectfully and accept a refusal. Seek information that could
change your plan, rather than only reassurance.

Cate Hall's 2024 essay on becoming more agentic also emphasizes
seeking feedback and making requests. It is useful practitioner advice, but it
is not an experiment demonstrating that its suggestions work for everyone.[^hall]

### 6. Use AI to support your thinking

Decide what you are trying to accomplish before asking for a long answer.
Use AI to propose options, explain a difficult point, critique a draft, or help
you plan a test. Then choose a way to check the important parts in the world.
For factual claims, that may mean inspecting an original source; for a workshop,
it means talking to participants.

When your goal is learning, try the problem yourself, ask for a hint or an
explanation, and reconstruct the solution without looking. This recommendation
is an extrapolation from the classroom experiment, not a tested universal
prompting formula.[^learning]

A possible instruction is:

> Help me think through this task. Ask what outcome I want, identify assumptions
> I should check, and suggest one manageable next step. If I am learning, give
> hints before a complete solution.

This is an illustrative prompt. Its value depends on what you subsequently do
and verify. Asking AI to praise your initiative does not tell you whether the
project is working.

### 7. Close the feedback loop

A meta analysis of 138 randomized studies involving 19,951 participants found
that interventions increasing progress monitoring improved goal attainment on
average. Effects were stronger in studies where progress was physically recorded
or reported to others, although those moderator comparisons do not independently
prove why the interventions worked.[^monitoring]

Try keeping three brief notes: **What did I attempt? What happened? What will I
change?** Review them at an interval that fits the project. You can share a useful
update with a trusted person; reporting progress need not mean publishing private
information.

Measure contact with reality. Invitations drafted, AI conversations opened,
and hours spent can all rise while the real goal remains untouched. Look for
evidence such as a clearer explanation, a participant's response, or a problem
you can now solve independently. Continue when the next attempt is worthwhile;
change course when the evidence or your priorities change.

### 8. Decide when to change the goal

Persistence needs a reason to continue. Wrosch and colleagues' three studies
found associations between capacities to disengage from unattainable goals,
engage with alternatives, and aspects of well-being. They compared measurements
over a limited period rather than testing a randomly assigned change. The
findings do not establish that quitting causes better well-being.[^adjustment]

A practical interpretation is to review the goal as well as the next step.
What evidence would justify continuing? Has the cost become unacceptable?
Could another approach serve the underlying value? A workshop might become
a short guide, or a project might pause while you seek support. Changing course
deliberately can be an exercise of agency; every obstacle does not require
more persistence.

## A worked example: feedback changes the plan

Return to the community workshop. This is a fictional teaching example;
its observations are invented to show how a decision can change.

| Stage | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Outcome | Find out whether a beginner workshop would help neighbours before committing to an event. |
| Constraint | You have two hours this week and cannot authorize a venue booking. |
| Attempt | Ask three people what format would help. Use AI to draft an invitation, remove an unconfirmed venue promise, and try a short explanation with one person. |
| Feedback | Two people prefer a one page guide; one wants a live session. The sample listener understands the definition but mistakes “superpower” for a guarantee of success. |
| Decision | Prepare a short guide, clarify that AI assistance creates possibilities rather than guaranteed results, and ask the listener to explain that distinction. Review whether the guide helps before expanding the project. |

Three conversations and one sample cannot establish wider demand or general
teaching effectiveness. They can inform this limited next decision. AI helped
with preparation; actual responses changed the format and explanation. The
useful result was a better justified next step, even though the original event
was not pursued.

## A two week practice experiment

The schedule below is a proposal from Latent Light, informed by the research
above. It has not been validated as a two week programme, and it does not promise
a personality transformation. Use it to explore one manageable goal.

| Period | Practice and record |
| --- | --- |
| Days 1–2 | Choose a useful outcome, identify one real constraint, and make a first attempt. Record your starting situation and what would count as progress. |
| Days 3–6 | Make further manageable attempts. Create an if–then response for a recurring obstacle. Record what you tried and what each attempt revealed. |
| Days 7–10 | Ask someone for substantive feedback or help. If using AI to learn, attempt the task without it afterward. Record what changed and what you can do yourself. |
| Days 11–14 | Compare the result with the original goal. Decide whether to continue, revise, seek support, or stop. Record the outcome, remaining uncertainty, and a justified next decision. |

You do not need an uninterrupted daily streak. Useful review questions are:
Did I reduce unnecessary delay before a first step? Did I test an assumption?
Did I ask for support that changed what was possible? Did the work benefit
anyone? These are informal reflection questions, not a validated agency scale.

## The environment matters

A workplace can praise initiative while making it costly to ask questions or
report mistakes. In a study of 51 manufacturing teams, Amy Edmondson found
that psychological safety was associated with learning behaviour. The study
combined surveys and qualitative research; it did not randomly assign teams
to a safety intervention.[^safety]

For a leader, a reasonable implication is to give people a clear outcome,
appropriate decision authority, access to resources, and a way to surface
uncertainty. Invite questions and discuss failed attempts without humiliation.
“Figure it out” becomes more useful when people have the means and permission
to do so. This is a practical interpretation of the research, not a proven
management package.[^safety]

For an individual, difficulty getting started can reflect unclear goals, a
missing skill, exhaustion, conflicting obligations, or a situation you cannot
change alone. Investigate those possibilities before turning the difficulty
into a judgment of your character. Agency is useful as a way to examine the
next choice available to you; it is a poor measure of a person's worth.

The durable practice is to choose deliberately, make an appropriate attempt,
learn what happened, and decide again. AI can help at several points in that
process. Developing agency means taking an active role in directing it.
