## The short answer

A complete application can recover reading order and layout, recognize tables
and formulas, split document packets, extract a typed schema, retrieve evidence,
answer questions and summarize. Business rules, uncertainty routing and human
review determine which results can move into a downstream system.

The field has moved from engineered OCR pipelines, through learned perception
and layout-aware pretraining, to OCR-free document models and modern
vision-language models (VLMs). These are overlapping eras rather than a clean
replacement sequence. Modular pipelines remain strong where coordinates,
determinism, latency, or local execution matter. VLMs adapt better to novel
layouts and flexible questions, but can omit, invent, or reorder content.

A useful production starting point is **adaptive and hybrid**: use native text or compact specialists for easy content, spend
high-resolution or general-VLM compute on difficult regions, and add source
grounding, validation, abstention, and risk-based review where errors are
costly. This is a design recommendation, not a universal benchmark finding.[^pure-doc-bench][^real5]

## What must be understood beyond the text

Two kinds of structure matter: **geometric structure** identifies regions on a page, while **logical structure** assigns
meaning such as heading, paragraph, table, field, or footnote.[^doclaynet]
Modern Document AI extends the logical layer into retrieval, reasoning, and
workflow action.

The evaluation unit changes at every layer. Character error rate does not
measure table structure; bounding-box accuracy does not measure reading order;
exact match does not fully measure semantic answers; and fluency does not
measure summary faithfulness. There is no meaningful “document accuracy” until
the task, corpus, metric, and cost of each error are specified.

Three input regimes also need different routing:

- **Born-digital documents** may contain cheap text and font information, but
  PDF object order need not match human reading order.
- **Scans and photographs** add skew, blur, shadows, warping, compression, and
  handwriting.
- **Mixed, visually rich documents** combine prose with tables, formulas,
  charts, stamps, signatures, and figures whose relationships carry meaning.

## How the technical stack evolved

### 1. OCR and engineered pipelines

Classical systems separated preprocessing, page segmentation, OCR,
reading-order heuristics, logical labeling, and export. Forms and invoices were
often handled with templates, anchors, coordinates, regular expressions, and
dictionaries. The pipeline was inspectable, but brittle when layouts,
languages, or capture conditions changed.

### 2. Learned perception and task-specific extraction

CNNs, sequence models, and object detectors improved recognition,
classification, region detection, and key-information extraction. Larger
datasets expanded layout diversity: DocLayNet provides 80,863 manually
annotated pages across 11 layout classes, and its baseline models remained
below inter-annotator agreement in the paper’s baseline experiments.[^doclaynet]

### 3. Layout-aware multimodal pretraining

LayoutLM jointly encoded text, 2-D coordinates, and visual appearance instead
of treating OCR output as a one-dimensional sentence. LayoutLMv3 later unified
text and image masking and added word–patch alignment.[^layoutlm][^layoutlmv3]
This encoder family remains useful for classification and token-level
extraction when OCR and boxes already exist, though it inherits upstream OCR
errors and often needs task-specific fine-tuning.

### 4. OCR-free generation

Donut reframed document understanding as image-to-sequence generation and
argued that an external OCR engine adds cost, rigidity, and error propagation.
Its synthetic document generator also anticipated the present importance of
controllable synthetic supervision.[^donut]

“OCR-free” does not mean that reading disappears—the model learns visual text
recognition internally. It changes the error boundary: instead of inspecting
OCR tokens and boxes, a decoder can omit, hallucinate, or structurally
misgenerate content. Later systems expanded image-to-sequence and instruction
formats across screenshots, documents, tables, charts, and QA.[^pix2struct][^udop]

### 5. Specialists, general VLMs, and hybrids

The current categories are complementary:

<FocusCanvas size="wide" label="Document AI approaches, strengths and weaknesses">
<ComparisonTable id="approach-comparison" title="Four complementary approaches" label="Document AI approaches, strengths and weaknesses" itemLabel="approaches">

| Approach | Main advantage | Main weakness | Strong fit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Modular pipeline | explicit artifacts, control, cheap native-text paths | brittle boundaries and error propagation | stable schemas, coordinates, local or low-latency systems |
| Document specialist | dense text and structure per unit of compute | narrower general reasoning | page conversion, tables, formulas, private serving |
| General VLM | flexible questions and broad language/visual knowledge | cost, hallucination, weaker diagnostics | unusual pages, ad hoc QA, cross-page reasoning |
| Adaptive hybrid | route each page or region to the right path | orchestration and evaluation complexity | heterogeneous production corpora |

</ComparisonTable>
</FocusCanvas>

**No universal SOTA.** OCR, layout detection, page conversion, information extraction, document QA,
and summarization use different datasets and metrics. Even for parsing,
rankings can change between clean digital pages and degraded scans. “Best”
only has meaning for a pinned task, corpus, model version, prompt, evaluator,
and operating constraint.

## The 2026 technical frontier

### Page parsing is becoming structured generation

Modern parsers increasingly output Markdown, HTML-like tokens, bounding boxes,
or typed document languages rather than plain text. SmolDocling is an especially
useful result: its 256M-parameter VLM emits DocTags encoding content, structure,
and location, showing how domain specialization can compensate for raw model
scale.[^smoldocling]

The recent releases change the system in four connected ways:

- **Allocate compute at inference.** Region parsers spend pixels on selected
  crops; Nemotron 3.5 Parse offers additional table/chart refinement passes;
  Unlimited-OCR 2 separately controls attended tokens and resident page
  memory. These are different accuracy, latency and context tradeoffs.[^mineru][^nemotron35][^unlimited2]
- **Train for usable structure.** olmOCR 2 supplies executable tests,
  Xiaomi-OCR-0 connects parsing with understanding tasks, and OCRia-1 rewards
  header–value associations and formatting. A better string match alone
  does not establish a usable table or field.[^olmocr2][^xiaomi][^ocria]
- **Assess a candidate separately.** Jev adds a released region verifier;
  its score still needs workload calibration, and it cannot recover a region
  that the parser never detected.[^jev]
- **Preserve more than a transcript.** DocTags, editable graphics and spatial
  or temporal records make representation part of the model contract.
  Retrieval can also search the page image directly, rather than relying on
  one flattened text view.[^smoldocling][^dotsmocr][^youtu][^colpali]

The persistent hard cases are reading order across columns and sidebars,
hierarchical or multi-page tables, chart geometry, small and multi-line
formulas, meaning-bearing formatting, and associations among captions,
footnotes, units, and objects.

In its May 2026 study, PureDocBench evaluated 40 systems on source-traceable clean, digitally
degraded, and physically degraded variants. Its best overall score was only
about 74/100; specialists no larger than 4B parameters could rival or exceed
general VLMs 5–100 times larger, while general VLMs sometimes degraded more
gracefully. No evaluated model exceeded 67% on the formula metric averaged
across the three tracks. These are results for that study’s tested models, not a ceiling on later releases.[^pure-doc-bench]

The maintained PureDocBench repository is a later artifact: its September 26
update covers 58 models, and an October 27 PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 pipeline rescore
uses explicitly recorded ground-truth and evaluator revisions. A current
leaderboard row and the original paper table must not be treated as the same
experiment. Pin the predictions, reference labels, rendering and scorer before
comparing a new result with an older one.[^puredoc-current]

## How to read the current model frontier

There is no useful single ordering of “the best OCR models.” The current
frontier mixes one-shot VLMs, layout-first systems, document converters,
reward-optimized parsers, and models that also answer questions or reconstruct
graphics. The more durable question is: **where does each design allocate
pixels, tokens, decoding time, and supervision?**

### Architecture primer

As an interface sketch, a document VLM can be written as

<MathExpression display number="1" tex="y = D\big(C(E_{\text{vision}}(I)),\ p\big)," />

where vision encoder <MathExpression tex="E_{\text{vision}}" /> turns page <MathExpression tex="I" /> into visual tokens,
connector <MathExpression tex="C" /> compresses or projects them into the language-model space,
prompt <MathExpression tex="p" /> selects the task, and decoder <MathExpression tex="D" /> generates structured output <MathExpression tex="y" />.
This sketch does not specify attention wiring: visual-prefix decoders and
cross-attending encoder–decoders implement the interface differently.
Models differ along four practical axes:

1. **Visual input:** fixed square, native-resolution page, dynamic tiles, or
   layout-selected crops.
2. **Token organization:** raster order, explicit layout order, or a learned
   semantic sequence.
3. **Generation:** ordinary autoregression, multiple tokens per step, or a
   draft-and-verify path.
4. **Output and supervision:** plain text, document markup, JSON, executable
   tests, or renderable graphics code.

Recurring building blocks include:

- **Native-resolution or NaViT encoding:** patch images at their natural aspect
  ratios instead of stretching every page into a fixed square.
- **2-D or multimodal RoPE:** encode row, column, and sometimes modality
  coordinates rather than only one text position.
- **Pixel shuffle, patch merger, or convolutional compressor:** merge nearby
  visual tokens so the decoder sees a shorter sequence.
- **Connector or projector:** map vision features into the decoder's embedding
  width, commonly with a small MLP.
- **MTP or speculative decoding:** advance several output tokens per expensive
  target-model step.
- **RL with verifiable rewards (RLVR):** optimize outputs using executable
  checks or task metrics rather than only next-token likelihood.

### A. End-to-end compression and visual reading flow

These models feed the page directly into one VLM. Their central problem is how
to retain small text and two-dimensional structure without flooding the
decoder with thousands of visual tokens.

#### SmolDocling: make the document language compact

SmolDocling uses a 93M SigLIP patch-16/512 visual backbone, aggressive pixel
shuffle and projection, and a 135M SmolLM2 decoder. Each 512×512 image becomes
64 visual tokens—4,096 pixels per token—bringing the total model to 256M
parameters.[^smoldocling]

It emits **DocTags**, an intermediate language with typed elements, nested
relationships, location tokens, and a table vocabulary. Docling later converts
that representation into Markdown, HTML, or JSON. Its design bet is that a
small model can perform conversion well when both its visual sequence and its
output language are carefully compressed. The tradeoff is specialization: it
is a converter, not a broad document-reasoning model.

#### Granite Docling: the next compact document-native converter

Granite Docling combines a SigLIP2 base patch-16/512 encoder, pixel-shuffle
projection and a Granite 165M text decoder for 258M parameters. Its training
first familiarizes the language model with DocTags, then teaches the full VLM
conversion using public and synthetic document data. It supports full-page
conversion, bounding-box-guided regions and structural questions. It is not
intended as a general image-understanding model.[^granite]

[Model and usage](https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-docling-258M) ·
[Docling integration](https://github.com/docling-project/docling)

The architectural continuity matters more than a size contest: the output
language makes location, type and structure explicit. Experimental language
coverage is not equivalent to validated quality on each language.[^granite]

#### DeepSeek-OCR 1: treat the page as optical context compression

DeepSeek-OCR asks how many visual tokens are needed to reconstruct much longer
text. Its 380M **DeepEncoder** first applies an 80M SAM-based window-attention
stage to many local patches, reduces their count 16× with two convolutional
layers, then applies a 300M CLIP-based global-attention stage. A 3B DeepSeek MoE
decoder activates roughly 570M parameters per token.[^deepseekocr]

Resolution modes trade fidelity for tokens: 512² and 640² inputs emit 64 and
100 tokens, while larger native or tiled modes emit hundreds to more than one
thousand. This is not lossless compression; the paper's own results degrade as
the text-to-vision-token ratio becomes extreme. Its contribution is a concrete
local-attention → compression → global-attention recipe for controlling visual
activation memory.

#### DeepSeek-OCR 2: learn a causal flow over visual evidence

**Motivation:** learn a useful sequence of visual representations before the
language decoder generates text. A document is two-dimensional; flattening
patches row by row need not match its logical reading order.[^deepseekocr2]

For example, imagine a two-column page. A raster sequence moves across both
columns, whereas a reader might finish the left column first. This is an
illustration of the ordering problem—not a claim that ordinary vision encoders
cannot understand columns or model global relationships.

Version 2 preserves the 80M tokenizer, 16× compression, and 3B MoE decoder, but
replaces CLIP with a Qwen2-0.5B **LM-style vision encoder**. Compressed visual
tokens attend bidirectionally. An equally large set of learned causal-flow
queries sees every visual token and only earlier queries; only those query
outputs are passed to the decoder.[^deepseekocr2]

Think of the queries as **learned reading slots**: each gathers page evidence
while incorporating earlier slots' representations. This is intuition, not a
guarantee that an individual slot corresponds to a particular document region.
The mechanism is not an explicit sorting algorithm or a discrete permutation
of patches. Query positions are processed in parallel through masked
transformer layers, rather than generated one at a time.[^deepseekocr2]

The intended effect is semantic ordering before language generation. With a
global view and zero to six local crops, the encoder sends 256–1,120 query
tokens to the decoder. “Reordering” is the authors' interpretation of the
learned query sequence—not a directly observed human reading path—but the
attention mask is precise:

<MathExpression display number="2" tex="M=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{1}_{m\times m} & \mathbf{0}_{m\times n}\\
\mathbf{1}_{n\times m} & \operatorname{LowerTri}(n)
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad n=m." />

The top-left block gives visual tokens full mutual visibility; the lower-right
block makes the flow queries causal. The decoder then performs a second causal
stage over the resulting sequence.

Equation (2) is a **visibility mask**: one means allowed and zero means
blocked. It is not the additive mask used directly in attention logits;
the additive equivalent uses zero for allowed entries and negative infinity
for blocked entries. The diagonal of the query block is allowed, so a query
can attend to itself as well as earlier queries.

**Takeaway:** the encoder organizes visual evidence into learned sequential
representations; the decoder turns those representations into text.

#### Unlimited-OCR: keep the reference, forget old output

Unlimited-OCR starts from DeepSeek-OCR's compressed **DeepEncoder** and 3B MoE
decoder, with about 500M parameters active during decoding. Its change is in
every decoder attention layer: standard full attention becomes **Reference
Sliding Window Attention (R-SWA)**.[^unlimitedocr]

[Figure 8](#figure-8) separates generation from cache maintenance. Eviction removes output history, not source evidence.

Read the example in two phases. While generating <MathExpression tex="y_t" />, the query reads the
entire permanent reference cache and the four recent output tokens. After <MathExpression tex="y_t" /> is
generated, the oldest output token is evicted and <MathExpression tex="y_t" /> is appended. The reference
cache does not move.

For a generated token at step <MathExpression tex="t" />, R-SWA exposes two kinds of memory:

<MathExpression display number="3" tex="\mathcal{N}(t)=\mathcal{P}\cup\mathcal{D}_n(t),
\qquad
C_{\text{R-SWA}}(T)=L_m+\min(n,T)\le L_m+n." />

<MathExpression tex="\mathcal{P}" /> is the complete fixed prefix—the prompt and compressed visual
tokens—and remains visible throughout generation. <MathExpression tex="\mathcal{D}_n(t)" /> contains
only the most recent <MathExpression tex="n" /> output tokens. Therefore the **decode-side** KV cache
stops growing after <MathExpression tex="n" /> tokens, while visual evidence is never evicted. Equation (3)
counts cached positions per attention layer, not memory bytes or model
weights. It is different from ordinary sliding-window attention, which could also push the
source image out of view.

This mechanism targets a different latency problem from GLM-OCR's MTP or
HunyuanOCR's DFlash: those methods try to emit several tokens per target-model
step, while R-SWA bounds how much past output each step must store and attend
to. The released model supports a 32K context and single- or multi-page
parsing through Transformers, vLLM, and SGLang.[^unlimitedocrrepo]

**“Unlimited” is the design direction, not a literal bound.** For a fixed visual prefix, R-SWA bounds cache growth with generated length.
But total cache is still <MathExpression tex="L_m+n" />, and <MathExpression tex="L_m" /> grows as more page images are
added. The report's 40+ page results use a small in-house benchmark, while
the released model remains constrained by its 32K context. Treat the title
as an aspiration toward long-horizon parsing, not proof of arbitrary-length
PDF support.[^unlimitedocr]

the pink area grows into a triangle; in R-SWA, it becomes a fixed-width

#### Unlimited-OCR 2: bound attention and resident pages separately

The October 27 release addresses the cost left open by R-SWA: even after old
output is evicted, every new token still reads all page references. Version 2
retains the DeepEncoder/MoE backbone and adds **Sparse Reference Sliding Window
Attention (SR-SWA)**. Its Page Gate restricts eligible visual blocks by page;
Token Sieve scores block summaries and selects a fixed top-k set. Attention
combines those selected blocks with a local token window. Selection is trained
through a routing bias in the attention logits, rather than a separate
dense-attention distillation objective.[^unlimited2]

#### HunyuanOCR-1.5: one model, long context, faster decoding

HunyuanOCR-1.5 retains the family's end-to-end path: native-resolution
Hunyuan-ViT → adaptive MLP connector → Hunyuan-0.5B decoder. Version 1.5 raises
the maximum image resolution from 2K to 4K and the context window to 128K so
the same model can handle document parsing, text spotting, extraction, QA,
translation, and multiple images.[^hunyuanocr15]

**What “native resolution” means.** The original HunyuanOCR report gives the
concrete lineage: Hunyuan-ViT is an approximately 0.4B visual encoder built on
SigLIP-v2-400M. Its adaptive patching preserves the input aspect ratio, and all
resulting patches pass through global ViT attention.[^hunyuanocr]

This differs from a conventional ViT pipeline that resizes every image to one
fixed square and therefore produces the same patch grid regardless of source
shape. Hunyuan-ViT accepts a variable-height, variable-width grid instead: a
long receipt remains tall and narrow, while a square page remains square.
Conceptually, for patch size <MathExpression tex="P" />:

Fixed-square ViT:

<MathExpression display number="4" tex="\begin{aligned}
H\times W &\rightarrow R\times R,\\
N_{\text{patch}} &= (R/P)^2.
\end{aligned}" />

Native-resolution ViT:

<MathExpression display number="5" tex="\begin{aligned}
H\times W &\rightarrow H'\times W',\\
H'/W' &\approx H/W,\\
N_{\text{patch}} &= (H'/P)(W'/P).
\end{aligned}" />

Preserving the page geometry helps retain small-character density, column
relationships, and table or chart structure. The resulting number of visual
tokens varies with the image; only **after** global vision attention does the
adaptive MLP connector pool and project those features into a shorter sequence
for the language model. The connector therefore reduces decoder-side visual
tokens, but it does not remove the vision encoder's cost of processing the
high-resolution patch grid.[^hunyuanocr]

This resembles NaViT's broader variable-resolution, aspect-ratio-preserving
principle, which HunyuanOCR-1.5 cites. But the original report does not describe
Hunyuan-ViT as simply NaViT: it specifically describes a SigLIP-v2-based
encoder with Hunyuan's adaptive patching and training recipe.[^navit][^hunyuanocr]

“Native” does **not** mean that every source pixel passes through unchanged or
that resolution is unlimited. Images may still be rescaled to a supported
pixel budget and rounded to patch-compatible dimensions. HunyuanOCR-1.5 raises
that supported envelope to 4K; the inspected image-processor configuration uses 16-pixel
patches and a 2× spatial merge. Treat processor pixel limits, vision config
fields and the paper’s supported envelope as separate contracts. The downstream Hunyuan-0.5B uses XD-RoPE to
assign text, height, width, and time coordinates to the resulting multimodal
sequence.[^hunyuanocr15][^hunyuanocr-config]

Its decoding addition is **DFlash**. A separate 90.7M, five-layer
block-diffusion draft model proposes up to a block of candidate tokens in one
parallel pass; the frozen target model verifies the block and accepts the
longest valid prefix. This preserves the target distribution while spending
otherwise idle compute to reduce sequential steps. The authors report 6.37×
Transformer and 2.14× vLLM speedups in their setup; gains depend strongly on
output length, regularity, batching, and serving conditions.[^hunyuanocr15]

#### Nemotron 3.5 Parse: inspect the attention path and inference passes

The October 17 release changes the version-specific lesson. Its approximately
0.631B model combines C-RADIOv4-SO400M, sequence compression and a ten-layer
mBART **causal visual-prefix decoder**. This is distinct from the
cross-attending Parse 2.0 described above. Its trained one-step MTP head is

plugin’s one-token speculative path are different serving contracts.[^nemotron35]

One pass parses the page. Two passes refine table/chart crops selected by that

More work can improve the authors’ ParseBench result while reducing throughput.
Their merge retains differing page/crop alternatives, and the reported scores

These systems use explicit page decomposition. The first stage simplifies the

#### OXR-1.0: make layout replaceable, retain recognition and order

OXR uses a separate, pluggable layout model. The OXR network then handles
content recognition and reading order as two branches, allowing a domain’s
region taxonomy to change without assigning recognition to a different
model. Its released implementation uses a SigLIP2/NaViT-style visual encoder
and a Qwen3 decoder. A learned visual vocabulary maps soft visual-token
distributions into embedding vectors for the language model; it is more
specific than an ordinary linear projection of image features.[^oxr]

The advertised size is 1.3B, while the inspected Hub metadata lists about
1.53B stored BF16 tensor values for the recognition checkpoint. Stored tensor
values are not an independently counted trainable-parameter total, and the
layout network is separate. Use the artifact and complete pipeline when
budgeting memory.[^oxr]

Its **recursive self-improvement (RSI)** is a post-training data loop. The
official diagram uses a general VLM to classify predictions, compares multiple
rollouts for disagreement, groups attributed errors, and generates new
image–label pairs matching reference styles. This describes targeted training
data construction, not automatic learning from every production document.
A formal report was still marked forthcoming at inspection, so the evidence
here is the release documentation and implementation, not a paper-level
ablation of RSI.[^oxr]

Weights are public, but the license is **Apache-2.0 with additional OXR
terms**, including commercial thresholds, service attribution and a competing
hosted-API restriction. Public weights do not imply unrestricted commercial
use.[^oxr-license]

#### Surya OCR 2: share recognition tasks without merging every stage

Surya OCR 2 uses a roughly 650M Qwen3.5-style VLM for recognition, layout and
table tasks. Prompts select layout JSON or full-page HTML behavior. Text-line
detection remains a separate small model, so “one shared VLM” does not mean
that every stage or dependency disappears. The surrounding package supplies
reading-order and table interfaces as well as recognition.[^surya2]

This is a useful modular option beside full-page converters. Test whether the
selected prompt and wrapper preserve the granularity your application needs,
especially table relations and region identity. Code and weights have separate
terms: the code is Apache-2.0, while the weights use a modified OpenRAIL-M
license with commercial conditions.[^surya2]

#### HPD-Parsing: parallelize the output tree

HPD-Parsing attacks the serial generation bottleneck differently. A main
layout branch establishes global structure and dynamically forks local content
branches that decode concurrently. Progressive multi-token prediction reduces
steps inside each branch. Unlike independent crop calls after an external
detector, the model’s decoding process coordinates the branching.[^hpd]

[Official usage and model link](https://www.paddleocr.ai/latest/en/version3.x/pipeline_usage/HPD-Parsing.html) ·
[Report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18839)

The documented runtime is a customized vLLM build supporting request forking
and P-MTP. Parallel decoding is therefore a model-plus-runtime property, not
something a generic Transformers loader automatically provides. Peak token
throughput is not document latency at your batch size.[^hpd]

#### MinerU-Diffusion: change the target decoder, not just its draft

The March 2026 report uses parallel block diffusion for document generation,
with uncertainty-aware curriculum learning and inverse-rendered data. Its
reported decoding acceleration is conditional on the tested setting; it is not
a guarantee for end-to-end ingestion.[^mineru-diffusion]

This differs from Hunyuan’s DFlash: DFlash proposes tokens that an
autoregressive target verifies. MinerU-Diffusion changes the target generation
method itself. The report is inspected; checkpoint availability was not
verified in this review.[^mineru-diffusion][^hunyuanocr15]

### C. Change the supervision or the output language

The next frontier is not only a better visual backbone. Some models change
what counts as a correct parse or expand what a parser is expected to emit.

#### olmOCR 2: executable document tests as rewards

olmOCR 2 starts from Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, fine-tunes on 267,962 pages, and
then applies GRPO on 2,186 synthetic pages with 30,381 HTML-derived binary
tests. Each page produces 28 completions; tests check content presence and
absence, reading order, table relations, formulas, and repetition.[^olmocr2]

For output <MathExpression tex="y" /> and <MathExpression tex="M" /> tests, the main reward is

<MathExpression display number="6" tex="R_{\text{doc}}(y)
=
\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\mathbf{1}[T_j(y)\text{ passes}]." />

This is more semantically tolerant than raw edit distance: equivalent table
serializations can pass while text in the wrong order fails. But tests only
reward properties they encode, and the paper's benchmark is English-only.

#### dots.mocr: treat graphics as programs, not leftover pixels

dots.mocr extends the output boundary beyond text-centric parsing. Its
scratch-trained 1.2B high-resolution encoder accepts up to roughly 11 million
pixels; a connector feeds a Qwen2.5-1.5B base decoder. The generated sequence
contains ordered typed regions <MathExpression tex="(\mathcal{B}_k,c_k,p_k)" />, where payload <MathExpression tex="p_k" />
can be text, table markup, LaTeX, or renderable SVG for charts, icons, diagrams,
and UI elements.[^dotsmocr]

The current release is not literally “parse everything in one pass.” Full-page
text parsing and region-level image-to-SVG decoding are task-conditioned
separate passes. SVG also introduces a different evaluation problem: many
programs can render the same picture, so render fidelity and structural checks
matter more than exact code match.

#### TeleOCR: geometry remains a frontier

**TeleOCR** adds deformation-aware learning, adaptive sampling for complex
layouts and content/structure decoupling for formulas and tables. The current
September revision uses the TeleOCR name; earlier indexing used NaviDC. Its
reported 78.41 PureDocBench score is a later author result, illustrating why
the original study’s roughly 74 score is not a current ceiling. Cross-report
scores still require matching evaluator and dataset versions.[^teleocr]

#### Xiaomi-OCR-0: connect reading to understanding

Xiaomi-OCR-0 starts from Qwen3.5-0.8B and trains one compact model for parsing,
key-information extraction and OCR-centric visual QA. Its report appeared
September 28; the official repository records the weight release on September
30. **Official checkpoints are available**, correcting this guide’s earlier
unverified availability status. The model and code carry Apache-2.0 terms;
community quantized conversions are separate artifacts whose benchmark parity
is not established here.[^xiaomi][^xiaomi-artifacts]

The main contribution is the relationship between **label quality, spatial
anchoring and understanding supervision**. Its approximately 170M-sample
corpus mixes regions, pages and understanding tasks. That is not 170M distinct
pages, and it is not a text-token count.[^xiaomi]

**Build labels, then decide what to learn from them.** A layout detector
supplies regions to heterogeneous expert recognizers. The pipeline ranks
experts by their mean pairwise agreement, keeps the top three, and selects the
most central candidate among that trio. Uncertain cases undergo
render-guided refinement and return to the expert pool for another consensus
check; selected unresolved cases receive human review. This is not an
exhaustive search over every possible expert triplet. Agreement is also not a
calibrated probability of truth: correlated experts can share an error.[^xiaomi]

Mining keeps two signals distinct: expert agreement estimates label
reliability, while the student’s consistency and errors indicate learning
need. Coverage-driven synthesis fills missing patterns; failure-driven
synthesis targets verified weaknesses. A frozen, source-group-separated
validation set guides recipe revisions but stays out of training and linked
synthetic derivatives. Because that set guides development, a separate sealed
test remains necessary for an unbiased final estimate.[^xiaomi]

1. **Q-Mask text anchoring.** A query-conditioned mask decoder adds spatial

3. **Mix-RL.** Task-specific rewards score text similarity, table structure,

**What do the experiments establish?** The authors report 96.83 on
OmniDocBench v1.6, 95.24 on Real5 and 87.94 on Wild. These headline parsing
results use **PP-DocLayoutV3 → crop recognition → page assembly**; the 0.8B
label does not include that detector. Direct whole-page parsing, KIE and VQA
are different operating paths.[^xiaomi][^xiaomi-artifacts]

The understanding-transfer ablation is more instructive than the ranking.
Adding understanding tasks slightly hurts an early region-only model
on OmniDocBench v1.6 (93.594 → 93.264), then helps after page-level parsing
training is established
(94.941 → 95.580). This supports a curriculum-dependent benefit, not “more
tasks always help.” Its separate Q-Mask and Mix-RL gains use different
training baselines and cannot simply be added. On the report’s 1,655-image
in-house KIE set, average accuracy is 59.09%, including 45.93% on addresses:
high parsing scores still leave substantial extraction failures.[^xiaomi]

#### WeVisDoc: use residual errors to allocate training

WeVisDoc-2B and 4B keep a Qwen3-VL-based end-to-end parser and change its data
curriculum. **The two stages are training stages, not a layout→recognition
inference pipeline.** Stage I expands semantic, structural and appearance
coverage, including degradations that preserve readable target content.
Stage II measures residual errors on a disjoint diagnostic probe within
fixed visual–structural clusters. Verified gaps guide new examples and
training-token exposure, with broad replay to reduce forgetting.[^wevisdoc]

The paper carefully distinguishes an under-covered cluster from a measured
failure: a group with no local probe evidence has a pooled prior, not an
observed error rate. Its 4B model reports 75.54 averaged across PureDocBench’s
three tracks and a 4.03-point Stage II gain on the Real Degraded track.
Stage II changes data, allocation and total training; that gain does not
isolate rebalancing under equal cumulative compute. Official 2B and 4B
checkpoints are released.[^wevisdoc]

#### OCRia-1: reward associations, not just recovered strings

LlamaIndex’s October OCRia-1 report starts from Qwen3.5-4B and concentrates on
merged headers, long tables, chart values and meaningful formatting. The
official announcement establishes use inside **LlamaParse**; a downloadable
public checkpoint was not verified. A reported model is not automatically a
self-hostable release.[^ocria][^ocria-release]

Its supervised dataset contains 100,979 pages, combining audited real pages
with synthetic documents whose source structure supplies exact targets.
Reinforcement learning uses 11,234 pages and 34 reward components, including
12 penalties. Rewards check such properties as whether a value is under its
correct header and whether chart values retain their series labels. Reward
tests found concrete shortcuts: an empty sequence can appear perfectly
ordered, and counting elements without checking their placement can reward
wrong output. Correctness needs content, placement and anti-omission checks
together.[^ocria]

The report’s serving result is a **system result**: a roughly 0.69B DFlash 2
draft, speculative block verification, mixed-precision weights and custom
vLLM/kernel work supplement the 4B target. Concurrency and draft acceptance
change the speed; a bare 4B checkpoint would not establish that latency.[^ocria]

Read its benchmark denominators carefully. The reported 87.4/100 ParseBench
average covers four dimensions and **excludes visual grounding**, because
OCRia-1 does not emit bounding boxes. The report’s main-table 90.5 olmOCR
score averages three table/formula categories, not the official eight-category
overall. Appendix D separately reports 83.5 overall at a 1,288-pixel long-side
rendering, in a comparison mixing author reruns and identified published
baselines. These are author evaluations, not a cross-model ranking reproduced
here. Old scans, some multilingual tables and output-budget-limited long
tables remain failure cases.[^ocria]

#### Youtu-Parsing-Omni: one grounded interface across modalities

Tencent’s October 29 report extends parsing beyond pages. The released 5B
model uses modality-specific input stems around a **shared perception
Transformer**, initialized from a text language model, then emits text through
a language decoder. Space–time positions and selected audio–video fusion
layers connect what is seen with what is heard. OmniSchema represents content
and its spatial or temporal evidence in one structured interface.[^youtu]

Its OmniSchema-Aware On-Policy Distillation (OSAD) trains on student-generated
prefixes. A frozen teacher sees the reference parse, while the student does
not; content and structure receive different token-level objectives. The
teacher refreshes between rounds. “Self-evolving” therefore still depends on
reference supervision. The report also says this larger autoregressive model
does not yet implement its predecessor’s inference acceleration. Treat it as
a broader perception option, with custom Youtu model terms, rather than an
automatic throughput upgrade for ordinary PDF OCR.[^youtu]

Across the families, the shared pattern is **capacity allocation rather than
parameter scaling alone**: decide which pixels become tokens, which tokens
reach the decoder, how many decoding steps are necessary, what structure the
output language makes explicit, and which errors the training signal can see.

### Extraction is becoming schema-conditioned generation

A common modern extraction path is:

1. parse the document into text plus structure;
2. supply a JSON schema, field descriptions, examples, or instructions;
3. generate typed values;
4. enforce structural validity;
5. attach each consequential value to a page or region;
6. normalize and check it against business rules or external data; and
7. abstain or route high-risk uncertainty to review.

Valid JSON is not evidence of a correct value. Stable, high-volume schemas can
still favor discriminative models with better latency and calibration.
Generative extraction is strongest when layouts and schemas vary or when a
field requires language understanding. A practical compromise uses a
generative extractor plus a smaller verifier, deterministic rules, or sampled
human review.

### QA still needs retrieval and evidence

DocVQA established single-page visual question answering with 50,000 questions
over more than 12,000 document images.[^docvqa] Real workloads are often
multi-page. MP-DocVQA therefore used hierarchical page representations—select
or compress pages before answer generation and identify the supporting
page.[^mpdocvqa]

The same idea underlies reliable multimodal RAG: parse evidence-bearing blocks,
retrieve documents then pages or regions, answer from the selected visual and
textual evidence, return citations, and support “not found.” Long context does
not remove retrieval; retrieval also allocates visual compute and creates an
audit interface.

Benchmark versions matter. MMLongBench-Doc-V2 corrected 106 annotations and
changed the evaluator to semantic equivalence; its authors explicitly state
that V2 results are not comparable with published V1 scores.[^mmlongbench]

### Retrieve the page image, not only its transcript

ColPali is an important complementary design. It embeds page images as multiple
vectors and uses late interaction between query-token and page vectors. This
lets retrieval retain layout and visual evidence that a plain OCR transcript
can discard.[^colpali]

For query vectors <MathExpression tex="q_i" /> and page vectors
<MathExpression tex="v_j" />, its MaxSim matching has the form:

<MathExpression display number="7" tex="s(q,p)=\sum_i\max_j q_i^{\top}v_j" />

Each query token finds its strongest page match; those matches are summed.
This is retrieval, not transcription or answer generation. A practical hybrid
can retrieve page images, then supply both the relevant image and typed parsed
blocks to an answer model. Multiple vectors also mean more index storage and
matching work than one vector per chunk; benchmark retrieval latency, recall
and downstream answer quality separately.[^colpali]

ColPali’s multi-vector approach is one option, not the only visual retrieval
interface. The October Nemotron 3.5 Embed release also accepts page images,
text or their combination. Its 1B-named variant returns one normalized
2,048-dimensional vector per input; a separate reranker can reorder the
retrieved candidates. Its model card gives 1.55B total parameters, another
reason not to read a size suffix as a full-pipeline memory budget.[^nemotron-retrieval]

The design decision is where to spend information and compute: compact dense
indexing, token-level late interaction, candidate reranking, then answer
verification. Compare recall, index size, retrieval/rerank latency and cited
answer quality on the same corpus. A retrieval score does not measure parse
fidelity or prove that a generated answer uses the evidence correctly.

See [multimodal integration](/architectures/evolution/multimodal-integration/)
for the underlying vision-language interfaces.

### Summarization is a downstream grounding problem

This review does not establish a standard visual-document summarization
leaderboard comparable to the parsing benchmarks discussed here. The critical work
is preserving section and table structure, retrieving complete evidence,
distinguishing observed facts from synthesis, citing pages, and detecting
omitted requirements. A good parse does not prove summary coverage, and a
fluent summary does not prove factuality.

## Evaluation is now a limiting layer

Choose the benchmark by the failure you need to expose. “Document AI score”
can mean transcription, spatial grounding, long-document continuity or finding
evidence for a question. Those are different experiments.

Public leaderboards can mislead through corpus mismatch, page-level metrics
that hide catastrophic field errors, benchmark corrections, contamination,
vendor-specific tuning, and a gap between digital pages and real capture.
PureDocBench reports 2,580 confirmed errors in 21,353 evaluator-scored
OmniDocBench blocks, or 12.08%; this denominator is not every annotated block
in the dataset. Real5-OmniDocBench separately reconstructs pages through scanning,
warping, screen photography, illumination changes, and skew to expose a
persistent physical-capture gap.[^pure-doc-bench][^real5]

Dr. DocBench adds expert-domain structures such as chemical formulas, music
notation, difficult tables and cross-page relationships. Its failure-based
selection is useful as a stress test, but does not estimate your production
error prevalence. Combine challenge sets with representative workload samples
instead of averaging them into one undifferentiated score.[^dr-docbench]

Long-document evaluation adds another axis. Unlimited OCR Works 2 introduces
MarathonDocBench: 100 documents spanning 103–300 pages, with cross-page text
and table continuations. This is an author-created stress test, not a
representative sample of all enterprise PDFs. Test page coverage, joins,
hierarchy and recovery after chunk boundaries separately from ordinary
single-page accuracy.[^unlimited2]

**OmniParseBench adds a useful October view.** Datalab publishes 16,288
pass/fail tests over 2,937 pages from 2,343 documents, with language, structure
and capture tags and a recorded derivation for each test. The headline
averages text, table and layout family scores; it is not the pass rate of all
tests pooled together. Missing layout output also changes which comparisons
are defined.[^omniparse][^datalab-open-bench]

Its open scorer makes failures inspectable, but this is a vendor-authored
benchmark that also compares the vendor’s own products. Test construction has
heterogeneous sources, including model-assisted verification. Deterministic
scoring does not make the reference labels infallible, and a test suite cannot
reward properties it never checks. Use its slices and released verdicts to
form workload hypotheses, then test those on independent documents.[^omniparse-data][^omniparse]

### Agree on the output before scoring it

Two transcripts can contain every printed word but encode different meaning:
a two-row table header flattened into one row can detach a value from its
year. LlamaIndex’s **Document Parsing Protocol (DPP), working draft 0.1**,
published October 28, makes such representation choices explicit. Its proposed
rules preserve spanning headers in HTML, distinguish blank form fields from
missing content, and retain chart data and readable diagram relationships.
It grew from ParseBench annotation guidance; it is a vendor-authored proposal,
not an adopted interoperability standard.[^dpp]

The general lesson is to version your own output contract before comparing
parsers. Decide which structures, locations and omissions matter, which
serializations are equivalent, and whether a benchmark actually scores them.
OCRia’s four-dimension ParseBench average, for example, cannot establish its
unmeasured visual grounding.[^ocria]

A production bake-off should use a rights-cleared, stratified sample of the
actual workload, including easy pages, rare but expensive layouts, bad scans,
handwriting, long packets, and “not present” cases. Keep a sealed test split and
measure the losses that matter:

- exact transcription for critical fields;
- reading order, table structure, and header–value association;
- field precision/recall, normalized match, and evidence coverage;
- document-level perfect-pass rate where every required field must be right;
- answer accuracy, abstention, and citation correctness for QA;
- factual consistency and required-fact coverage for summaries;
- straight-through processing rate and review minutes at a fixed quality bar;
- p50/p95 latency, throughput, retries, and fully loaded cost; and
- every metric sliced by document type, language, capture condition, and
  parser/model version.

Vendor confidence is not automatically a calibrated probability. Estimate
empirical error by field and slice, and price false values, missing values,
unnecessary reviews, and delays separately.

### MiniCPM-OCR Jev: score the candidate against the crop

Jev is a released **2B verifier**, trained from Qwen3.5-2B with a binary
classification head replacing the language-model output head. It takes three
matched inputs: the original region image, candidate OCR text, and an image
rendered from that candidate. It returns a 0–1 score for text, table or formula
regions. It evaluates existing candidates; it does not transcribe a page or
repair a region that every parser missed.[^jev]

The service contract makes an important distinction: only `verdict: scored`

interpreted as low-quality OCR. Pin the renderer as well as the model;
fonts, browser and formula/table rendering change the verifier’s input.[^jev]

The authors report **93.6 AUROC ×100** on JevOCRBench. That measures how well
scores rank correct versus incorrect candidates; it is not 93.6% accuracy or
proof that a 0.9 score means 90% correctness. A cross-parser check on the same
200 sampled regions reports AUROC between 91.9 and 93.4, including Xiaomi,
GLM and PaddleOCR outputs. It does not establish one threshold for every
document distribution.[^jev]

For deployment, label representative workload regions, choose a threshold
using both accepted-result accuracy and acceptance volume, then test it on
independent data. Audit randomly sampled accepted regions too: reviewing only
low scores cannot reveal the verifier’s false accepts. Parser and verifier
errors can correlate, and a region scorer does not measure page-level detector
recall. These are separate production checks, not capabilities established by
the benchmark.

### Validating a model-based judge

An LLM judge is not ground truth. Published evaluations identify position,
verbosity, and self-enhancement biases; agreement on one benchmark does not
establish reliability for document extraction or figure summaries.[^judge-validation]

**A proposed document-specific checklist:** define correctness, coverage, and conciseness
separately; build a human-reviewed sample across relevant document slices;
inspect false accepts and false rejects, not just average agreement. For paired
comparisons, swap answer order to check sensitivity. Include fluent but wrong
outputs and terse but correct ones. Fix the judge version and rubric when
comparing systems, and recheck reliability after either changes.

Separate the measurements: summary rubrics assess the summary; direct QA tests
answerability from the supplied representation; RAG adds retrieval and context
selection. A downstream gain does not identify which stage improved without
additional comparisons. For synthetic training data, judge scores are an
intermediate check, not a substitute for held-out model evaluation.

## A dependable production architecture

1. **Ingest and route.** Preserve the source, verify file and page metadata,
   detect native text, classify and split packets, and route by language,
   quality, complexity, and governance boundary.
2. **Parse with evidence.** Keep blocks, coordinates, reading order, hierarchy,
   tables, formulas, figures, source text, and parser version. Flat Markdown is
   convenient but may discard information needed for review or reprocessing.
3. **Execute a typed task.** Use a schema for extraction, an evidence plan for
   QA, or a coverage rubric for summarization. Separate observed values from
   normalization, inference, and arithmetic.
4. **Validate and abstain.** Combine schema checks, ranges, totals, cross-field
   consistency, master data, grounding coverage, capture risk, and calibrated
   thresholds. A region verifier can score a matched candidate, but failed
   scoring and missing detections need separate handling.
5. **Review by risk.** Send people the fields where expected error cost exceeds
   review cost, with the source crop and reason for escalation—not every page.
6. **Monitor outcomes.** Track input drift, versions, validation failures,
   corrections, downstream rejection, cost, and latency. Canary changes and
   keep rollback paths. Track parse completion, dropped regions, truncated
   output and cross-page joins as well as field errors.

Agreement between two models is not proof: they can have correlated failures.
Grounding is not proof either, but it makes a mistake inspectable. Reliability
comes from the complete control loop.

For a new parser, record **four contracts** in the deployment manifest: the
model/checkpoint; the SDK, renderer and decoding mode; the output schema and
normalization rules; and the evaluation plus acceptance policy. This is a
proposed operating practice. It keeps optional draft models, extra passes,
changed confidence semantics and revised label sets from silently changing
what an “accepted document” means.

### Treat document content as data, not authority

A document can contain instructions aimed at the model, including text hidden
in a PDF or embedded in retrieved content. Indirect prompt-injection research
shows why retrieved data must not acquire the privileges of system
instructions.[^injection]

The following are system-design recommendations:

- Enforce access rules before retrieval and when assembling evidence. Apply
  the same rules to crops, summaries, extracted fields and indexes.
- Keep source content separate from tool permissions. A page may suggest an
  action, but it cannot authorize a write, payment or external message.
- Validate files and sanitize generated HTML or SVG. Do not execute embedded
  scripts or fetch arbitrary URLs while rendering a parse.
- Define retention, deletion, redaction and outbound-service rules for
  originals and every derived representation.

Grounded output can still disclose restricted information. Accuracy and
authorization are separate tests. See [Security & Control](/agents/security-control/).

## What comes next

### Directions already visible in released systems

- **Compact models and adaptive serving.** Small cores can be paired with
  crop refinement, draft decoding or bounded attention; the complete serving
  path determines the resource tradeoff.[^minicpm][^nemotron35][^unlimited2]
- **Data and rewards that target remaining errors.** Xiaomi, WeVisDoc and
  OCRia illustrate different ways to choose useful supervision and preserve
  structure. Their results do not isolate every ingredient.[^xiaomi][^wevisdoc][^ocria]
- **Separate generation, verification and acceptance.** Jev makes the first
  separation explicit; workload tests and review policy still own the final
  decision.[^jev]
- **Representations and retrieval that retain visual evidence.** Typed
  documents, page-image embeddings and grounded records widen the options
  beyond one Markdown transcript.[^granite][^nemotron-retrieval][^youtu]
- **More inspectable evaluation.** Revised reference labels, tagged unit tests
  and published output contracts make the scorer itself part of the versioned
  system.[^puredoc-current][^omniparse][^dpp]

### Plausible, but dependent on disciplined execution

- Agentic workflows can choose parsers, refine schemas, query systems, and
  reconcile discrepancies—but only bounded tools, typed state, evidence, and
  acceptance tests make those loops safer.
- Multimodal retrieval will increasingly retain page images, structure,
  tables, and multiple representations rather than only text chunks.
- Evaluation will become continuous and slice-aware, with regression tests
  generated from corrections.
- Selective specialist/general-model ensembles may help, but their correlated
  failures and added cost must be measured.

More uncertain are one universal document foundation model replacing most
pipelines, self-improving agents without governed test gates, and a confidence
score that transfers reliably across corpora and tasks.

## Working conclusion

The durable unit of comparison is the **complete document workflow**:
what evidence it reads, what structure it preserves, how much work its serving
path performs, and which errors its acceptance policy allows through.
Recent releases improve different parts of that workflow. Their headline
scores do not collapse those parts into one interchangeable capability.

Choose the output contract and error cost first. Establish a cheap baseline,
compare specialists and general models on the same workload slices, and test
retrieval, candidate verification and human review where they change the
result. Keep the source evidence and version every contract that affects an
accepted document. Optimize for information a downstream user can verify and
use.
