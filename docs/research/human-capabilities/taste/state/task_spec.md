# Taste

## Inquiry

What is taste as a human capability, why does it matter with generative AI, how can it develop, and where do claims about it overreach?

## Intended reader and use

Curious adults who create, select or commission writing, design, products and AI-assisted work. Readers need a concrete understanding and practices they can try, without a universal ranking of people.

Learning goal: distinguish preference, aesthetic appreciation, domain expertise and evaluative judgment; assess the AI-era argument; learn a realistic approach to noticing, comparing, making and revising work.

## Scope and time boundary

Scope: historical and contemporary meanings; cognitive and social accounts of appreciation; contextual standards and expertise; AI generation and selection; development practices and evidence limits.

Evidence boundary: foundational research and primary work, plus current discussion and AI evaluations inspected October 11, 2026 (Vancouver). Record versions and distinguish experiments, theoretical models, essays, commercial claims and original synthesis.

## Exclusions

Exclude food or sensory taste, universal personality scores, luxury consumption advice, claims of guaranteed career protection, an exhaustive history of aesthetics, and reproduction of copyrighted example artwork. Do not equate a social group's preferences with universal superiority.

## Research map

- Meaning and standards: historical arguments, practitioner essays and distinctions between preference and evaluative judgment.
- Cognitive mechanisms: aesthetic processing, perceptual learning, fluency, familiarity and expertise.
- Social context: status cues, social influence, shared standards and legitimate disagreement.
- AI era: creative output, diversity, selection and domain-specific model evaluation.
- Development: comparative judgment, feedback, making and reflective practice; distinguish studied components from a proposed taste-training programme.
- Skeptical direction: taste as vague status signaling; impressive appearance versus usefulness; expert disagreement; AI learning evaluative preferences; limits of transferring narrow tasks to broad creative competence.
