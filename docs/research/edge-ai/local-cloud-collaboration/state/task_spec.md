# Local and Cloud Model Collaboration

## Inquiry

How can local specialists and stronger cloud models cooperate to improve useful task outcomes under quality, latency, cost, privacy and device constraints?

## Intended reader and use

AI researchers and engineers familiar with LLMs/VLMs but new to device and cloud cooperation.

Learning goal: distinguish cooperation mechanisms, inspect their real evidence,
and design an evaluation that can establish useful gains for a domain.

## Scope and time boundary

Inference collaboration between local models and stronger remote models,
with selected foundational and recent primary work available by October 17, 2026.
Cover routing, cascades, task decomposition, specialist evidence, advisory
inference, speculative decoding and neural-network splitting. Distinguish
online cooperation from offline distillation and learning loops.

Evidence boundary: inspected original reports, official project records and
current first-party architecture documentation. Product disclosures establish
architecture, not independent effectiveness or deployment share.

## Exclusions

No employer-specific work, exhaustive paper catalog, device benchmarks,
model training, framework installation, external service changes, commit,
push or deployment. No inferred production adoption from academic citations.

## Research map

- Taxonomy and history: original routing, cascade, splitting and decoding papers.
- Collaboration mechanisms: Minions, advisor/local–remote orchestration and relevant recent work.
- Real systems: official Apple and robotics architectures, without assuming one uniform deployment.
- Systems tradeoffs: device runtime, network rounds, cold start, battery, disclosure and failure behavior.
- Domain design: document/visual evidence and continuous perception, explicitly proposed rather than measured.
- Evaluation: matched local/cloud baselines, routing oracles, evidence completeness and realistic network conditions.
- Skeptical direction: cloud-only can be simpler and faster; local intermediates can lose evidence;
  self-confidence is not calibrated; remote-token savings are not total-system savings;
  compressed representations and divided computation do not guarantee privacy.
